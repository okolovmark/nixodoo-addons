The AI Assistant: Claude answers an employee's question from live Odoo data,
which it looks up through read-only tools that run as that employee. Every
internal user has it, in two places:

* **Ask AI**, a button in the chatter of a record: answers about that record
  and the records linked to it, as internal notes on the record;
* the **AI Assistant** app: conversations on a full page or in a quick panel
  from the systray, and AI reports.

It only reads: it creates, changes and deletes no record and never offers
to. The one thing it saves is an AI report, the employee's own page. It
answers on Claude only, the models of ``claude_models`` in order (Sonnet 5.5,
then Haiku 5.5), never on another model: when no Claude model answers,
nobody does. It answers in the language of the question, gives times in the
employee's timezone and names the records its answer rests on.

**Ask AI**

*Ask AI* sits after *Log note* in the chatter of a saved record (an icon on a
phone). It opens a dialog for a question of up to 2,000 characters. The
question is posted at once as a note by the employee; a queue job writes the
answer as a second note by *AI Assistant*, in reply to the question, opening
with a line that quotes the asker and the start of the question: two answers
on one record may arrive in either order. Both carry the *Ask AI* subtype,
which is internal and followed by nobody: they notify nobody, and every
internal user who can see the record sees them. The asker does not become a
follower.

Ask AI answers about the record and the records linked to it; for reports,
rankings and statistics over other records it points to the app. It is
refused on a record the employee may not read, on one the AI deny list holds,
on a Discuss channel (its notes would reach the members) and on the
assistants' own ``ai.*`` records. A missing, hidden or denied record gets the
same message.

The answer is escaped, then only its marks are rendered (bold, emphasis,
code, headings, lists, paragraphs); only URLs into this Odoo, at its own host
or a path of the web client, become links. A question without an answer gets
a note instead:

* it needed more tool rounds or time than one answer has: *narrow it (one
  record, a shorter period) and ask again*;
* no Claude model answered: *the AI Assistant is not available right now*.

The record's earlier Ask AI questions, from any asker, go with a new
question as its history (see How an answer is made), as long as the
question note and the note of its latest answer are still in the chatter.
Another employee's earlier question is passed with their name, quoted as
data, never as an instruction.

The asker rates an answer with a thumbs-up or thumbs-down reaction on the
answer note, also on a record they may only read. Only the asker's own
reactions count; a thumbs-down wins over a thumbs-up.

**The AI Assistant app**

The *AI Assistant* app opens on *Assistant*, which lists the employee's
conversations, the most recently active first, with *New chat* and *My
reports*; administrators also have *Review* and *Configuration* (see
Administration). A conversation is named after its first
question and kept until its owner deletes it, which deletes its questions
and answers; the AI reports it saved stay in *My reports*. An employee sees
only their own conversations.

The magic-wand icon in the systray opens a quick panel over the current
screen and carries it: the window action's model and, on a form, the record
shown. The panel continues the employee's latest conversation while it was
asked from the same screen; otherwise the next question starts a new one.
*Open in the AI Assistant app* moves to the full page. A question in the app
may be about any data the employee can see: the screen is context, not a
limit. While an answer is written the composer waits; each answer has
*Helpful* and *Not helpful* buttons, and the same button again takes the
rating back. A new answer an administrator sends shows in the conversation
as an answer quoting the question it answers again (see To Review).

**How an answer is made**

``readme/answer_flow.puml`` draws one question.

A question is an ``ai.assistant.exchange`` row, answered by one queue job on
the channel ``root.ai_assistant``. The row keeps the companies selected in
the switcher when the question was asked, the current one first; the answer
reads in those the employee still works in, or in their main company when
none is left. When the job runs, the employee's group and, for Ask AI, their
access to the record and the deny list are checked again. A question refused
then is closed as *Unavailable* without asking Claude: nothing is posted on
the record, and an Ask AI asker alone gets a notification that they no longer
have access to the AI Assistant or to the record; in the app the refusal shows
in place of the answer.

The job sends Claude the system prompt (a constant of the module), the
history, and a context block (the employee, the date and time in their
timezone, their companies, the record or screen) before the question, then
runs the tool loop of ``ai.claude.gateway._claude_chain``
(``ai_claude``). Each model has ``claude_max_tool_rounds`` (12)
rounds of tool calls and ``claude_tool_budget_seconds`` (300) from its first
call; a tool asked for past the budget is not run. A model that reaches
either limit ends the question without an answer: the next model would meet
the same limit.

The history is the last 10 answered questions asked before the question,
oldest first: the record's for Ask AI, the conversation's in the app. Each
goes with its latest answer: a new answer takes the place of the answer it
answers again.

The job's own transaction writes nothing; the answer is written in a short
transaction of its own, only while the question is still pending, so a
second run of the job never answers twice. That write is tried 3 times when
it meets concurrent updates; then one more transaction closes the question
as *Unavailable*, with the *not available* text, without asking Claude
again. Should that meet a conflict too, the question stays pending for the
cron below.

An answer that says neither the module documentation nor the data answers
the question is flagged *Not covered*. A question whose job failed or was
cancelled is closed at once as *Unavailable*, and the asker sees the
*not available* text. The cron *AI Assistant: close questions whose answer
never came* (every 5 minutes) closes the questions still pending
``pending_stale_seconds`` (1800) after their job started or was enqueued, or
after the question when it has no job; a running job is never cut short.
Whichever of these closes a question, an app asker who has lost the AI
Assistant since sees the refusal instead; for a new answer the refusal is
told to nobody.

**Tools**

The tools run as the asking employee, in the companies of the question, and
refuse to run as the superuser: the employee's access (``ir.access``),
companies and field groups decide what they return, as they do on screen,
then the AI deny list takes away what the assistant must not read.
``find_models`` reads the model and field lists (``ir.model``,
``ir.model.fields``) as superuser, then keeps only the models the employee
may read and the fields their field groups let them see, less the deny list.
``find_menus`` reads the menus, their actions, the access lines and the
roles as superuser and returns only names from them; whether the employee can
open a menu is decided as the employee. ``find_models``, ``find_menus`` and
``module_docs`` read the installed modules behind a model as superuser too.
Each call runs in a savepoint that is always rolled back, under a statement
timeout of ``tool_seconds`` (15).

* ``search_read``: the records matching a domain with the fields asked for
  (none asked: the stored fields that are not binary, HTML, JSON,
  properties or x2many), at
  most ``max_rows`` (200); it says when more match.
* ``read``: records by id, archived ones too, at most ``max_rows``; it lists
  the ids that do not exist or that the employee may not see.
* ``read_group``: totals by group. Group keys are fields of the model itself,
  a date by day, week, month, quarter or year; measures are
  ``field:function`` or ``name:function(field)`` with sum, avg, min, max,
  count or count_distinct of a stored field; every group has ``__count``; at
  most ``max_rows`` groups.
* ``find_models``: where a kind of data is kept, for a query of a few words,
  such as *leave credit*. The query is split at spaces, commas, semicolons
  and ``/``, and each word loses the punctuation at its ends (an inner ``.``
  or ``_`` stays, as in ``hr.employee.credits``). Single characters and
  common question words, such as how, many or left, are not looked for.
  ``no`` is looked for only right after a word that is, as in *serial no*,
  not in *why is there no attendance*. A note names the words not looked for
  (*Not looked for: why, is, there, no.*); of more than 10 words, only the
  first 10 are looked for, and a note says so.

  A model matches when its technical name, its description or the label of
  one of its fields holds a word of the query, ignoring case. A word of two
  letters, such as OT or SL, matches only a whole word; a longer one matches
  only where a word starts (after anything but a letter or a digit), so
  *tag* finds *Tags* but not *Stage*. A word of four letters or more that
  ends in s is also looked for as a singular: *-ies* becomes *-y* from five
  letters on, *-sses*, *-xes*, *-ches* and *-shes* lose *es*, any other *-s*
  but *-ss* loses its *s*. ``_`` and ``%`` are plain characters. The fields
  every model has (``id``, ``create_uid``, ``create_date``, ``write_uid``,
  ``write_date``, ``display_name``) are never matched by label, and a model
  that only ``no`` matches is left out.

  It returns at most 15 models: those matching more of the words other than
  ``no`` first, then those whose name or description holds more of them as
  typed, then those whose name or description holds any of them, then those
  whose description ends with one of them (*Public Employee* before
  *Employee Location*), then those with a label holding ``no``, then those
  with more matching fields, then by technical name. Each comes with its description, up to 10 matching fields
  (technical name and label, those matching more words first) with the
  count of the others, and the installed modules that define or
  extend it and have a README, which ``module_docs`` reads. Left out: models
  the employee may not read, models on the AI deny
  list, transient and abstract models, and fields outside the employee's
  field groups or on the deny list. A note also gives the count of matching
  models past 15, or, when none matches, suggests another word, a synonym or
  the singular.
* ``find_menus``: where a screen, menu or report is, for a query of a few
  words, such as *inventory analysis*. The query is read and its words match
  as for ``find_models``, against the menu's name, its action's name and the
  names of the menus above it, in the employee's language; a menu that only
  ``no`` matches is left out.

  It looks at the active menus whose action exists. Left out: menus without
  an action and menus under an archived one; developer mode menus (whose
  only group is ``base.group_no_one``) and the menus Odoo keeps out of the
  employee's web client whatever their access, with the menus under them.

  It returns at most 10 menus: those matching more of the words other than
  ``no`` first, then those matching more of them in their own or action
  name, then by path; a note gives the count past 10, or, when none matches,
  suggests another word, a synonym or the singular. Each comes with:

  * ``path``: the menu names from the app down, joined by ``>``;
  * ``model``: the model its window, server, report or client action opens,
    when that model is installed; a URL action has none;
  * ``modules``: as for ``find_models``; or instead ``denied``, when the
    model is on the AI deny list or a wizard's, whose data and documentation
    the assistant does not read;
  * ``can_open``: whether the employee can open the menu and every menu above
    it outside developer mode: the groups of each, and read access to the
    model of the menu's own window, server or report action.

  A menu the employee cannot open also has ``needs``, lists of group full
  names such as *Accounting / Invoicing*: every list is required, one group
  of a list is enough. A list holds the groups of a menu on the path the
  employee has none of, or the groups whose access lines read the model of
  the menu's own window, server or report action when the employee may not
  read it; a list holding every group of another is left out. ``needs`` is
  absent when no group opens the menu, as when no access line lets anyone
  read such a model. With base_user_role installed, ``roles`` lists up to 5
  roles, by name, that each grant a group of every list on their own, and
  ``more_roles`` counts the others.
* ``fields``: the fields the employee may see and the deny list leaves, with
  label, type, related model, help, selection values and whether they can be
  searched, sorted and grouped.
* ``record_history``: a record's chatter, newest first, without user
  notifications: author, date, type, subtype, text (each cut at 2,000
  characters), attachment names and tracked changes, read from the
  message's text where Odoo writes them, at most ``max_rows`` messages,
  marked as untrusted data.
* ``module_docs``: the READMEs of the installed modules that define or
  extend a model, by name; a module may read others, or fewer, through
  ``ai.assistant.tools._module_docs_modules``. Each README
  is the running module's rendered one, as plain text, without its table of
  contents, changelog, bug tracker, credits, link targets and media.
* ``report_run``, ``report_save`` and ``report_get`` (app only): see *AI
  reports*.

A tool result holds at most 60,000 characters: ``search_read``, ``read``,
``read_group``, ``find_models`` and ``find_menus`` drop rows from the end,
``fields`` drops fields and ``record_history`` the oldest messages, each
with a note saying so. HTML comes as plain text, datetimes in UTC. ``module_docs`` shares one
result among a model's READMEs as evenly as their lengths allow; the module that
defines the model, and those ``_docs_floor_module_names`` adds, keep at least
a quarter each. A README cut to fit ends at a paragraph with ``[cut at X of
its Y characters]``, a closing note names the modules cut or left out, and
the tool with a ``module`` and an ``offset`` reads that README alone from X
on.

A search of messages (``mail.message``) that holds them to no model, or to
more than 5, reads the matches in its order, the newest first by default,
1,000 at a time, each batch checked as Odoo checks which messages the
employee may read, up to 10,000 matches: past them ``search_read`` says it
looked at the first 10,000 only and ``read_group`` refuses, both asking to
name the model.

The model reads errors as text written for it, never Odoo's own message,
which may name records read as superuser: a refused access is *Not
available: the employee may not read this*, a query past the timeout asks to
narrow it, an unexpected failure names only its type.

A model that is not installed is not a refusal: the error says it is not
installed here and that the data may be kept in a model of a custom module,
to look for with ``find_models``. The system prompt says so too; a module may
say more there through ``ai.assistant.engine._system_prompt_parts``. An
abstract model gets an error that it holds no records, also pointing to
``find_models``. Both errors are logged as *Error*, not *Denied*.

The system prompt also tells the model never to guess a model: it finds one
with ``find_models``, or with ``find_menus`` when the question is where
something is in Odoo, before it reads that model's documentation or data.

**Domains**

A domain is a JSON array of ``[field, operator, value]`` terms and the prefix
operators ``&``, ``|`` and ``!``. Every field of a path must be one the
employee may read and the deny list leaves. Refused, with a reason the model
can act on:

* ``=?``, and ``in`` or ``not in`` without a list (one id on a link is taken
  as a list of it);
* a computed field Odoo cannot search;
* ``child_of`` and ``parent_of`` on anything but ids, or on a link Odoo
  cannot walk. They take ``id`` or a stored, non-related link of the model's
  own, or one inherited from a parent that stores it, to its own model or to
  a model whose hierarchy is stored or held by a plain link to itself; never
  messages. They start only from roots the employee may read and the deny
  list leaves, and Odoo resolves them with its own rules;
* a path through a link to messages that Odoo searches with each message's
  access, or such a link compared with a name: Odoo would check every
  message one by one;
* comparing as a whole, rather than by a field of its rows, a one2many whose
  inverse Odoo computes rather than stores, as an analytic account's lines;
* grouping by a path; sorting by a field Odoo cannot sort, grouping by one
  it cannot group, aggregating one it does not store; and sorting, grouping
  or aggregating (other than counting) a message's text or another HTML
  text, or an employee's cost or a total of it where a row holds one (see
  AI deny list);
* filtering, sorting, grouping or aggregating (other than with ``count``) by
  structured data: a JSON, properties or properties definition field
  (``additional_identifiers`` of a contact, ``task_properties``), which
  Odoo would compare as stored while the tools show its URL tokens redacted
  (see AI deny list). A JSON or properties definition value may be tested
  set or not (``=`` or ``!=`` false), and an ``analytic_distribution`` is
  filtered by its analytic accounts, their ids or names, as Odoo does; to
  count by analytic account, the refusal points to the analytic lines'
  ``account_id``;
* comparing a stored text (a char, text or HTML field) or a message's text
  with ``<``, ``>``, ``<=`` or ``>=``, any filter on a mail's own copy of
  its text, and a name filter on a model whose name search reaches a hidden
  field, or searches in its own code a text the tools show otherwise than
  stored, or in code the deny list does not know (see AI deny list);
* reading a field by a dotted name, and reading a binary field.

Without an order, a search keeps the model's own. A path through a one2many
whose inverse Odoo computes, as an analytic account's lines, which it finds
by analytic plan, is followed through every stored link of its rows back to
the record, whatever the plan.

One exception follows Odoo's own search: on a path through a link Odoo
searches past its rows' access (a record's messages or activities, a user's
contact), and on a term that compares a link the deny list filters with
ids, a range, a name search's result or nothing, the linked rows are matched
in SQL past their access, as Odoo matches them or compares a many2one's
column. The deny list's row filter and the link's own domain still apply,
and every later step of the path is resolved with the employee's access.

**AI deny list**

What the assistant never reads, even when the employee may: an Ask AI answer
is seen by everyone who can see the record, not only by the asker.

* **Models** (fnmatch patterns): ``hr.payslip*``, ``hr.payroll.*``,
  ``hr.contract*``, ``hr.version`` (an employee's contract and private
  data), ``hr.appraisal*``, ``res.partner.bank``, ``res.bank``,
  ``ir.config_parameter``, ``ir.mail_server``, ``fetchmail.server``,
  ``res.users.apikeys*``, ``auth_totp.device``, ``payment.provider``,
  ``payment.token``, ``mail.ice.server``, ``mail.guest``,
  ``mail.push.device``, ``mail.push``, ``certificate.*`` (keys and
  certificates, whose files an attachment's indexed content would quote),
  ``sign.request.item.value``, ``sign.log`` and ``ai.*``; and every
  transient model, since settings and wizard rows hold what was typed in
  them.
* **Fields**: the hourly ``cost`` and ``display_cost`` of a project's
  employee mapping; ``account_number`` of bank statement lines; the task
  brief (``brief`` of tasks, from ``project_ai_driven``), the whole task in
  one text, its notes, their authors and its attachments included, past the
  masks applied value by value: the assistant reads a task field by field;
  ``can_open_last_appraisal`` on any model (``hr_appraisal_oca``'s flag on
  employees and their public profiles), a bit derived from the appraisals, a
  denied model; the website's Plausible and Google Maps keys, a signature
  request's share link, a Discuss channel's ``uuid`` and invitation URL, the
  live chat report's copy of that ``uuid``, an
  automation rule's webhook URL (``base.automation.url``) and a server
  action's (``ir.actions.server.webhook_url``); a calendar event's meeting
  URL (``videocall_location``), which ends with the event's access token; a

  ``attendance_kiosk_key`` and ``attendance_kiosk_url``, which open the

  an HTML text kept as diffs, which no mask of the current text reaches.
* **Employees**: every field of ``hr.employee`` that ``hr.employee.public``
  does not have, but those ``ai.assistant.tools._employee_work_fields``
  names, and every ``hr.employee`` field restricted to a group of ``hr`` or
  of an ``hr_*`` module. A field an employee shows from its ``hr.version``
  goes by this rule alone, and a user's field that shows the namesake field
  of their employee goes with it.
* **Employee requests**, the models ``ai.assistant.tools._request_models``
  names (none here): readable, but none of their attachments, those posted
  or scheduled in their chatter included, and none of their mails, which
  print the reason. Notes, tracked changes and comments without a file stay
  readable; their reason fields go on the deny list with them.
* **Employees' costs**: an amount that is hours times an employee's hourly
  cost, or a total of such amounts, reads *an employee's cost, on the AI
  deny list* on the rows that hold one, matches no filter there, negated or
  not, and is no sort key, group or total over rows that hold one. The other
  rows keep their values. These are:

  * the ``amount`` of an analytic line that names an employee (a
    timesheet); lines without an employee (accounting) keep theirs;
  * the ``amount`` and, with ``sale_timesheet``, the ``margin`` (the revenue
    plus that cost) of a *Timesheets Analysis* row
    (``timesheets.analysis.report``) that names an employee, and the
    ``timesheets_cost``, ``attendance_cost`` and ``cost_difference`` of a
    *Timesheet Attendance* row (``hr.timesheet.attendance.report``);
  * the ``balance``, ``debit`` and ``credit`` of an analytic account, and a
    project's ``analytic_account_balance``, when the account has a line that
    names an employee, even one the asker may not read: beside the hours
    they would give the rate. They are computed, so no filter, group or
    aggregate takes them, and a sort by one over such an account is refused;
  * with ``sale_timesheet``, a sale order line with a timesheet: its
    ``purchase_price``, which ``sale_timesheet_margin`` sets to the
    timesheets' hourly cost, ``margin`` and ``margin_percent``; its order's
    ``margin``, ``margin_percent`` and ``extra_total_fields`` (the totals
    under the order, the margin among them); and the ``purchase_price``,
    ``margin`` and ``margin_percent`` of a *Sales Analysis* row
    (``sale.report``) of such an order;
  * a project's ``estimated_cost`` and ``estimated_cost_ratio``
    (``sale_project_margin``) when a sale line it bills has a timesheet.
    They are computed, never a filter, sort key or group: only their value
    is masked.
* **Links** into a denied model, and the fields that copy a denied field: a

  namesake field one link away. Copies of the VAT number go with it, denied
  with it and hidden from an employee who may not read it: a contact's
  ``routing_endpoint`` (``account_edi_ubl_cii``), the VAT number unless
  another identifier, and its ``routing_identifier``; and every text or JSON
  field a model computes from its own ``vat``, as a localization shows the

  of ``l10n_se``, ``l10n_in``'s GST state warning, whose state the number
  codes, ``l10n_nl``'s ``available_additional_identifiers_metadata``,
  whose placeholder for the OB number is cut from it). The display name and
  the kinds of identification number
  (``l10n_ar_afip_code``, ``l10n_pe_sunat_id_code``,
  ``l10n_co_dian_id_code``) show no number and stay.
* **Names**: a model whose record name (``_rec_name``) is a denied field,

  name of its records and of every link to them, the record line of a
  question's context included, and a ``read_group`` key by ``id`` or by a

  a call session of such a member, and a group chat that has a guest among

  not masked (see Roadmap). A display name Odoo builds from a denied field
  that is no link, as a product's from its internal reference with

  ``_rec_name`` does instead (*Desk* for *[DESK-01] Desk*) where it shows
  that field's value, in the same places, and so does a display name that
  shows it through a name it is built from, as an invoice line's that
  shows its product's code. A name shows a value where it holds the
  value's string form in any case, an empty value aside; a name that
  shows none stays as Odoo builds it: with ``res.partner.vat`` and
  ``res.partner.email`` hidden, a contact still reads *Acme, Bob*, as Odoo
  adds the number or the address only with a context key. A
  ``_rec_name`` that is a many2one reads that record's name as the tools
  show it; a model without a ``_rec_name``, or with one of another link,
  and a record whose ``_rec_name`` is empty, as an invoice line Odoo names
  by its product, or holds one of the values its display name shows, as a
  contact named by its hidden email address or an invoice line labelled
  *[DESK-01] Desk*, read *a name on the AI deny list*. So does a
  record whose name is also built from a denied record. The author of a
  message in a record's history reads the same. A model whose name search
  reaches a field the deny list hides or the employee may not read refuses
  such a filter too: with ``res.partner.vat`` hidden, a name filter on contacts, or on a
  link to a contact, is refused, since Odoo matches a contact's name with
  its VAT number. The fields a name search reads are ``_rec_name``,
  ``_rec_names_search`` and the name searches of the links among them, and
  for a model that searches names in its own code, those
  ``NAME_SEARCH_FIELDS`` declares for it: a user's login, a product's code
  and barcode, an account's code and description, a contact tag's parent
  (the search adds its subtags). Such a model also refuses a name filter
  while one of these texts, in any of its rows, is shown otherwise than
  stored (a URL token, a masked HTML text), since its code matches the
  stored text, and a model whose own name search the list does not declare
  refuses it always. The refusal names ``complete_name`` and ``name`` to
  filter on instead, through the link the filter crosses
  (``partner_id.name``); ids still compare, and the names still show.
* **Rows of a denied record**: its messages, posted or scheduled (with the
  files Odoo keeps under a scheduled one until it posts it), their
  translations and SMS, its mails, tracking values, notifications,
  reactions, followers, activities, attachments, calendar events and
  ratings, and the ratings of a record under it (a task's under its
  project). Also
  left out: attachments that hold a denied binary field or whose
  ``res_model`` names no model; a link to a refused attachment reads *an
  attachment on the AI deny list*.
* **Message text**: a message, posted or scheduled, loses its text, which
  then matches no filter, when it carries a refused attachment, when its
  body links to a record of a denied model (account's note on a contact
  that one of its bank accounts was created), when it holds a Discuss
  meeting's URL (*Contact Attendees* posts it on the event) or when it
  shows the number, as stored or sanitized, of a bank account its own
  record links to (an invoice's mail prints the number of the invoice's
  bank account). The copies of its text lose theirs with it: its mails,
  translations and SMS, a mail group message's body, a rating's feedback, a
  visitor's answer to a chatbot (``chatbot.message``), and a record's
  ``rating_last_feedback``, masked when any of the record's ratings is. An
  SMS, a rating or a chatbot answer without a message goes by its own
  text: a link to a denied record or a meeting's URL.
* **HTML text**: any other HTML field (a task's description, an
  activity's note, a contact's notes) that links to a record of a denied
  model or holds a Discuss meeting's URL reads *Left out: the text links a
  record or holds a URL on the AI deny list.* and matches no filter, and
  so does such an HTML value of a properties field. No HTML field is a sort
  or group key, nor aggregated other than counted.
* **URL tokens**: in every text the tools show, the value of each token in
  a URL reads ``[token-on-the-AI-deny-list]``: in a message's text and its
  copies, in a record's own text fields and the names of its links (a
  task's description copied from the email that created it, an activity's
  note and feedback, a lead's name and chat history), in ``read_group``
  keys, in every text a properties or JSON value holds, in
  ``record_history`` (the old and new values of tracked changes, the
  author, the subtype, the attachments' names) and in a question's
  context. A token is the value of a parameter whose name ends in
  ``token`` (``access_token``, ``token``) after ``?``, ``&``, ``;`` or
  ``#``, with its separator and ``=`` percent-encoded or not
  (``%3Faccess_token%3D``); the rest of a URL past a path that carries
  one, a record id before the token included (a Discuss meeting's, an
  event's calendar file's, a survey's, a rating's, a Discuss invitation's,
  an automation's webhook, the attendance kiosk's, a shared dashboard's,
  or one a module adds through ``_url_token_paths``); and a portal link's
  signature, its ``hash`` in a URL that carries the partner's ``pid``,
  percent-encoded or not (``%26pid%3D5%26hash%3D``; a ``hash`` without
  ``pid``, as in a file's or an asset's URL, stays). A parameter's name, a
  path and a value run on across inline tags, ``<wbr>`` and comments, as
  a browser shows them (``?access_<b>token</b>=``,
  ``/survey/<b>…</b>/start``); a value ends at any space, a no-break
  space included. In an HTML text a value also runs on across a character
  reference, as a browser decodes it, but one to a character that ends
  it: ``&``, ``#``, ``<``, ``>``, ``"``, ``'``, a space, a tab, a line feed
  or a Unicode space (``&amp;``, ``&#38;``, ``&#35;``, ``&#32;``,
  ``&nbsp;``, ``&ensp;``). A value runs on across a ``?`` (``&#63;``), and
  across a reference to a carriage return, a form feed or a vertical tab
  (``&#13;``, ``&#12;``, ``&#11;``), which ``html2plaintext`` drops; the
  character itself ends it. Each character of a parameter's name, of
  the separator before ``pid`` or ``hash`` (``&#38pid``, ``&#37;26hash``),
  of ``=`` or ``%3D``, of ``pid``, ``hash`` or a path may be a character
  reference, decimal or hex, with leading zeros or none and ``;`` or none, as lxml
  and a browser decode one, or a named one, in any case (``tok&#101;n``,
  ``&#x3d;``, ``&#37;3D``, ``/survey&#47;``, ``&equals;``, ``&sol;``); a
  reference to another character, as ``&#610;``, is none of them. A char
  or text value is read as it is, references included. A text filter
  matches the text with each token's value replaced by one vertical tab,
  so what it finds never depends on what a token holds: the words on
  either side of a token match, joined only by a ``_``, a ``%`` or a
  vertical tab of the filter's value (with ``unaccent``, also a sign it
  turns into one, as ``＿`` or ``％``), or by the whole placeholder
  ``[token-on-the-AI-deny-list]``, of any case for an ``ilike``, which the
  filter reads as that vertical tab; each meets any token alike. So ``=``
  or ``in`` with a char or text value as shown, or with an HTML value's
  stored HTML holding the placeholder, finds it, and ``!=`` or ``not in``
  the other texts. A word of the placeholder (*on-the-AI*) matches nothing. A
  placeholder a text holds outside a token, as an answer that quotes a
  redacted text does, is text like any other: its words match, the whole
  placeholder of a filter's value does not. A filter on a display name,
  or on a link compared with a name, matches alike for a model that
  searches names by its ``_rec_names_search`` or ``_rec_name``; one that
  searches names in its own code is refused while a text it reads is
  shown redacted (see Names). No filter compares a stored text that may
  hold a token (a char, text or HTML field) with ``<``, ``>``, ``<=`` or
  ``>=``, which would sort the texts, and none reads a mail's own copy of
  its text
  (``body_html`` and ``body_content`` of ``mail.mail``), which would scan
  the whole HTML of every mail: the refusal points to
  ``body``, the text of the mail's message, or to the mail's subject,
  recipients, state and dates. Structured data (a JSON, properties or
  properties definition field) is read only: no filter, sort key, group or
  aggregate but a count, which Odoo would compare as stored, tokens
  included; a filter only tests a JSON or properties definition value set
  or not, and an analytic distribution is filtered by its accounts. The
  ``fields`` tool marks such a field neither sortable nor groupable, nor
  searchable but an analytic distribution.
* **Link previews**: Odoo keeps one link preview (``mail.link.preview``)
  per URL, tied to each message that holds it by a
  ``mail.message.link.preview`` row, and lets an ERP manager read them all.
  The tools return a preview and such a row only for the messages the
  employee may read and whose text they show; a link to another reads *a
  link preview of a message the AI deny list leaves out*, and a list of
  them leaves it out. They look among the newest 10,000 messages with a
  link, as Odoo checks one by one which messages the employee may read:
  past them, a result that left out a preview of an older message, or a
  row of one, says so; in an AI report, only the dataset that left one out
  says it has more, and a dataset without a limit still runs.
* **Tracked changes**: Odoo writes them into the message's text, each field
  named by its label. Hidden are the changes of a denied field, of a field
  the employee may not read, and of any field of a denied model. Such a
  field's label, in any installed language, hides a change on the messages
  of its own model, on those of every model linked with it by a relational
  field either way that tracks no readable field of that label (Odoo logs a
  record's changes on a linked one, as a bank account's on its holder), and
  on a message without a model. ``record_history`` leaves out those change
  lines, and a tracking message that names such a label loses its text (of
  the message, its mails and its translations). A VAT number goes by every
  country's label of it, as Odoo names it after its company's country.
  With ``mail_tracking`` installed, its tracking values of these fields stay
  out too.

``deny_models_extra`` and ``deny_fields_extra`` add patterns, and so may a
module through ``_deny_list_models`` and ``_deny_list_fields``; narrowing
the list is a code change. A refused call is logged as *Denied*, and the model is
told the data is not available and not to reach it another way.

**AI reports**

In the app, a request for a report, a chart or a table to keep gets an AI
report (``ai.report``). ``readme/ai_report_flow.puml`` draws its life. The
model writes:

* the **specification**: up to 8 datasets, each grouped (group keys and
  measures, as ``read_group``) or a list of records (fields, as
  ``search_read``) over a model and a domain, with an order and a limit; up to
  20 formula columns per dataset or merge, over numbers, the columns before
  it, ``+ - * / %``, comparisons and ``abs``, ``round``, ``min``, ``max`` and
  ``coalesce`` (parsed and walked, never run as code; a null operand or a
  division by zero gives null); and up to 4 merges of two grouped datasets on
  their group keys (``inner``, ``left`` or ``full``), whose keys must be of
  the same kind;
* the **page**: HTML and CSS with its written conclusions, and bindings that
  tie page elements, by id, to the data: single figures (the first, last,
  sum, average, minimum, maximum or count of a column, optionally of the rows
  matching a value), tables (columns, a limit, a total row) and charts of
  Odoo's Chart.js 4 (bar, horizontal bar, line, pie, doughnut, stacked or
  not).

``report_run`` runs the specification as the employee through the same tools
and returns all its data; past ``report_max_tokens`` (about two characters of
JSON a token) it refuses and asks to aggregate further, as it does when a
dataset without a limit matches more than ``max_rows``. ``report_save`` runs
it again and keeps the data with the page and bindings. The model types no
figure into the page except in the conclusions, which quote what
``report_run`` returned. When a model saved a report and the next model
starts over, that model's first save replaces it. ``report_get`` returns a
report the employee may open, as untrusted data, to save a revised version as
a new report; a report a colleague shared comes without its specification.

The page keeps only its layout and text: headings, paragraphs, sections,
lists, tables and inline formatting, ``<style>`` at the top of the page, and
the attributes ``id``, ``class``, ``style``, ``title``, ``lang``, ``dir``,
``align``, ``width``, ``aria-*`` and, on cells, ``colspan`` and ``rowspan``.
Scripts, event handlers, images, SVG, forms and every link's ``href`` are
removed; a style sheet or style attribute whose CSS loads a file or a font or
runs script (``url()``, ``image-set()``, ``@import``, ``@font-face``,
``expression()``, ``-moz-binding``, ``behavior``) is removed whole. A tag
removed leaves its content in place; the cleaning runs again on its own
output until its parser (libxml2) gives it back unchanged, three passes at
most. A page that is mostly script is refused. A title has up to 120
characters, a page up to 100,000.

The app shows the page in a sandboxed frame that holds a second sandboxed
frame with the page, both under a content security policy that refuses every
request and lets only Chart.js and the module's renderer
(``static/report_frame/report_renderer.js``) run, by their hashes. The
renderer fills the bindings from the stored data. A figure over a dataset
whose limit left rows out is marked *(partial)*, a table or chart over one
says it holds the first rows, in the reader's language: the server sends
these notes translated with the data.

The data is a snapshot taken when the report was saved. *Refresh*, for the
owner only, reruns the specification as the owner, without the model, in the
report's companies the owner still works in, the current one first; the page
and its conclusions stay as written, and the report then says they were
written on older data. A refresh that fails says why as the tools tell the
model: their own reasons as written, a refused access, a timeout or an
unexpected error by kind only (logged as a warning), never Odoo's message. A
model the report reads that is no longer installed is told in plain words:
*the model X it reads is not installed any more*. A failed refresh keeps
the report's data. *Export* downloads an xlsx file: a sheet of the report's facts, then a sheet
per dataset and merge.

*Share* lets the owner choose colleagues who use the AI Assistant. They get
the report itself, the owner's snapshot: title, owner, dates, page and data,
which they may export, but neither refresh nor share. The conversation and
answer it came from, whom it is shared with, its specification and its
companies are for administrators only, and nobody else filters, sorts or
groups reports by them; the owner sees whom it is shared with in the app, and
the AI Assistant reads the specification for them with ``report_get``. Each
share and unshare is logged (by whom, with whom, when, and the report's
title), and the log outlives the report. The owner and administrators may
delete a report; it is then gone for everyone it was shared with.

**Administration**

For Settings administrators the app has two more menus. Each window is
titled as its menu.

*Review*:

* *To Review*: the answers to review, Ask AI and app alike (see To
  Review);
* *Conversations*: the conversations of every assistant (filters *Ask AI*,
  *AI Assistant*): one Ask AI conversation per record,
  shared by its askers, and one per app conversation. Each shows its
  transcript, its answers with their cost and attempts, and a chatter of
  deviations: a fallback to the next model, a question no Claude model
  answered, a spend limit. *Not helpful*, the stored count of answers their
  askers rated down, is a column of the list, a figure on an AI Assistant
  conversation's form and a filter; with *With fallbacks* also on, the list
  holds the conversations that are both;
* *Assistant Answers*: every question with its kind (*Ask AI* or *AI
  Assistant*), employee, record or screen, companies, state (*Pending*,
  *Answered*, *Unavailable*), feedback, *Not covered*, review (*Fixed*,
  *Dismissed*), model, tokens, cost, latency, attempts and the AI reports
  it saved;
* *Tool Calls*: every tool call with its employee, AI conversation, tool,
  model, arguments, rows returned, the modules whose README text
  ``module_docs`` returned, outcome (*OK*, *Denied*, *Error*), error and
  latency, never the data it read. A row is written in a transaction of its
  own, so it outlives a failed answer, and is deleted after 12 months;
* *Reports*: every AI report, with its share log.

*Configuration*:

* *Settings*: the parameters of the AI Assistant, but the runtime state
  (see Configuration).

A module of another assistant may add its own screens to these menus.

The cron *AI Assistant: watch for Claude going quiet*, every 15 minutes,
runs the Claude incident check of ``ai_claude`` for the AI Assistant: when
Claude answered none of at least 3 questions in 30 minutes, it mails the
incident recipients, and again when Claude answers. A question that ran out
of tool rounds or time counts for neither: Claude answered, the question
needed more lookups. The AI Assistant has a Claude circuit breaker of its
own.

**To Review**

An answer is to review while its asker rated it down, it is flagged *Not
covered* or it was not answered (*Unavailable*), and no administrator has
closed it. *Review > To Review* lists these answers, the newest first;
*Assistant Answers* has the same filter, *To Review*, next to *Fixed* and
*Dismissed*. An answer's form shows *To Review*, *Review*, *Reviewed by*
and *Reviewed on*, the answer it is a new answer to, and the new answers
sent to it.

The review buttons are for Settings administrators. *Fixed* and *Dismiss*,
on the form of an answer to review or on a selection in the list, close
the selected answers still to review as *Fixed* or *Dismissed*, keeping who
and when; the others are left as they are. Nothing reopens a closed
answer.

*Send new answer*, on the form of an answer with a thumbs-down, *Not
covered* or not answered, to review or closed, asks the AI Assistant the
question again after a confirmation. It is refused with the reason, and
nothing changes, while a new answer to the question is still being
written, or when the asker is archived, no longer has the *AI Assistant*
group, no longer has any of the companies the question was asked in or,
for Ask AI, may no longer Ask AI on the record (it was deleted, they may no
longer read it, or the AI deny list holds it). Otherwise the answer is
marked *Fixed* if it is still to review (a *Dismissed* one stays so), and
the new answer is queued to run as the asker: in their language and
timezone, with their rights, on the same record or screen, in the
companies of the question they still have, with the history from before
the question.

The new answer replies to the question:

* Ask AI: a note by *AI Assistant* in reply to the question note, opening
  with *New answer to the question of*, the asker and the start of the
  question;
* app: an answer in the conversation, without the question repeated,
  opening with *New answer to your question of*, the date it was asked and
  the start of the question. The conversation's transcript marks it *New
  answer to the question of* that date.

Once it is answered, the asker alone is notified, in Odoo (*Notifications*
in the messaging menu) or by email: for Ask AI by the note itself, for the
app by a note of *AI Assistant* on the conversation with the link *Open it
in the AI Assistant*
(``/odoo/action-ai_assistant.app?conversation_id=<id>&menu_id=<menu>``).
The *View* button of that mail and the notification open the app on the
conversation too; should its owner open the conversation's
form, it shows neither its figures nor its transcript and answers. A new
answer that is not answered notifies nobody and shows as any unanswered
question does; one refused, however it is closed, posts nothing and tells
nobody. Either way it is to review.

A new answer is an answer like any other: it may be rated, reviewed and
answered again; a new answer to a new answer quotes the question the asker
asked. Later questions carry the new answer in their history, not the
answer it replaced.

The cron *AI Assistant: weekly digest of answers to review* mails
``digest_recipients`` every answer to review, whatever its age, at most 50,
the newest first. Those created in the last 7 days are marked *New*; the
mail counts them, the answers to review and the questions asked in those 7
days, where a new answer an administrator sent never counts as a question
asked. Each row gives the date, the asker, the kind (for a new answer, with
the date of the question it answers), why it is to review, the record or
screen, the modules whose documentation the answer read, links to the
answer (*Review*, ``/odoo/ai.assistant.exchange/<id>``) and to its AI
conversation (``/odoo/ai.conversation/<id>``), then the question and the
answer or the error. Nothing is sent while no answer is to review and no
question was asked in the last 7 days; without recipients the digest is
only logged.

**A module of the organisation's own**

* ``ai.assistant.tools``: ``_deny_list_models`` and ``_deny_list_fields``

Installation
============

Install, from the OCA, ``queue_job`` (``OCA/queue``) and ``report_xlsx``
(``OCA/reporting-engine``), both of branch 20.0, and the Python packages
``anthropic`` and ``httpx2``. ``ai_claude``, of this repository, is installed
with the module. ``test-requirements.txt`` at the root of the repository
lists them as pip requirements, with the versions the tests run with.

Answers run as jobs of ``queue_job``: run its job runner as its README says,
and give the job channel a capacity (see Configuration).

Put an Anthropic API key in the environment of the Odoo service, in the
variable ``ai_assistant.claude_key_env`` names (``ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`` as
installed), and restart the service. The key is never stored in the
database; while the variable is unset or empty, the AI Assistant does not
answer.

Configuration
=============

*Internal User* implies the group *AI Assistant / User*
(``ai_assistant.group_ai_assistant``, the one group of the privilege *AI
Assistant*): every internal user has the app, the systray panel and *Ask
AI*. The app's *Review* and *Configuration* menus, the *Fixed*, *Dismiss*
and *Send new answer* buttons, every conversation and every AI report are
for Settings administrators.

The module's ``ir.access`` lines set what each reads: an employee reads and
deletes their own AI Assistant conversations, reads their own AI reports and
those shared with them, deletes their own, and reads the share log of their
own; a Settings administrator reads and deletes every conversation, answer,
tool call and AI report, and reads every share log.

*AI Assistant > Configuration > Settings* opens the *AI Assistant* block of
the Settings app, also listed there. Its section *AI Assistant* sets the
parameters below, prefix ``ai_assistant.``. A value in brackets is installed
with the module only: an update neither brings back an emptied parameter
nor adds one new to the data file, which takes a migration. An emptied
text field or effort removes its parameter; its default then applies and
the form shows it: the installed value, but nothing for ``claude_models``
and ``claude_key_env``. A number must be above zero: the form refuses 0 or
less, and an emptied number field is 0. A number parameter set in *System
Parameters* to 0 or less, or to anything but a whole number (any number for
``tool_seconds``), is read as its default, and a ``claude_effort`` the API
does not accept as medium; the form shows what is read. Saving any Settings
page writes the default shown for an absent parameter.

* *Claude*: ``claude_models`` (``claude-sonnet-5-5,claude-haiku-5-5``), the
  Claude models, comma-separated, asked in order; empty, the AI Assistant
  does not answer. ``claude_key_env`` (``ANTHROPIC_API_KEY``), the
  name of the environment variable of the Odoo service that holds the
  Anthropic API key; the key is never stored in the database. While the
  variable is unset or empty, the AI Assistant does not answer.
  ``claude_effort`` (medium; low, medium, high, xhigh or max),
  ``claude_max_tokens`` (16000) and ``claude_timeout_seconds`` (120): per
  Claude call. ``claude_max_tokens`` caps the whole reply, an AI report's
  page and bindings included.
* *Tools*: ``claude_max_tool_rounds`` (12) and
  ``claude_tool_budget_seconds`` (300), one model's tool loop;
  ``tool_seconds`` (15), the statement timeout of one tool call, fractions
  allowed; ``max_rows`` (200), the most rows or groups one tool call
  returns, and the most a report dataset holds.
* *Circuit breaker*: ``circuit_threshold`` and ``circuit_cooldown_seconds``,
  the AI Assistant's Claude breaker, not installed; absent, 3 failed
  questions in a row and 300 seconds.
* *Answers and AI reports*: ``pending_stale_seconds`` (1800), a question
  still pending this long is closed as *Unavailable*;
  ``report_max_tokens`` (200000), the most data, in estimated tokens, the
  report tools hand the model.
* *AI deny list*: ``deny_models_extra`` and ``deny_fields_extra`` (empty),
  comma-separated fnmatch patterns added to the AI deny list, models as
  ``x_salary*`` and fields as ``model.field``, such as
  ``sale.order.x_margin*``. They only add to the list.
* *Notifications*: ``incident_emails`` (empty), recipients of the mails on
  Claude not answering the AI Assistant, empty: nobody is mailed
  (``ai.assistant.exchange._incident_recipients`` may name others);
  ``digest_recipients`` (empty),
  recipients of the weekly digest, empty: it is only written to the server
  log. Both comma-separated.

The breaker and incident state, ``claude_consecutive_failures``,
``claude_circuit_open_until`` and ``claude_incident_open``, is written by
the module and is in *Settings > Technical > Parameters > System
Parameters* (developer mode) only.

Set the variable ``claude_key_env`` names in the environment of the Odoo
service to a key of a workspace of the Anthropic Console of its own. That
workspace's monthly spend limit caps what the AI Assistant costs; Odoo keeps
no budget of its own. A module that installs after this one may name
another variable in ``ai.assistant.engine._default_claude_key_env``. Only
``ai.assistant.engine._seed_claude_key_env`` reads it, and sets the
parameter to it while the parameter still holds ``ANTHROPIC_API_KEY``: it
takes effect only where that module calls ``_seed_claude_key_env``, for
example from a noupdate ``<function>`` of its data, which runs at its
install only.

Answers run as queue jobs on the channel ``root.ai_assistant``. Give it a
capacity in the job runner's ``channels`` line of ``odoo.conf``, for example
``root.ai_assistant:2``: it caps how many answers wait on Claude at once.

A neutralized database has ``claude_models``, ``digest_recipients`` and
``incident_emails`` blanked, so a copy of production never calls Claude,
whatever key its host's environment holds, and mails nobody.

Usage
=====

**About one record.** Open it, click *Ask AI* in its chatter, after *Log
note* (a wand icon on a phone), write the question and click *Ask*. The question shows in the chatter at once and the
answer note follows when it is written, as a reply to the question that
opens by quoting the asker and the question, so two answers on one record
are not mixed up. React with a thumbs-up or thumbs-down on the answer to
rate it. A later question on the same record sees the record's last 10
answered Ask AI questions with their latest answers, so a follow-up may
build on them. Everyone who can see the
record sees both notes. If, when the answer is due, you no longer have
access to the AI Assistant or to the record, nothing is posted: a
notification tells you alone that the question was not answered.

**About anything else.** Open the *AI Assistant* app, or click the
magic-wand icon in the systray to ask about the screen you are on without
leaving it; on a phone the app shows the list of conversations and a
conversation in turn. Ask in any language. The AI Assistant reads what you could look
up yourself in Odoo, less the AI deny list; when it says it cannot see
something, the data is outside what you may read or on the deny list. Ask
it where a screen, menu or report is: it finds the menu, says whether you
can open it and, when you cannot, which groups or roles open it. If you
lose the AI Assistant while a question waits, the refusal shows in place of
its answer.

**AI reports.** In the app, ask for a report, a chart or a table to keep.
The answer shows a card that opens the report; *My reports* lists your
reports and those shared with you. To change one, ask for the change in its
conversation: the AI Assistant saves a revised version as a new report. In a
report, *Refresh* computes the figures again from today's data (the written
conclusions stay as they were; when a model the report reads is no longer
installed, it says so and the report keeps its data), *Share* shows it to chosen colleagues,
*Export* downloads its data as xlsx, and the bin deletes it, for everyone it
is shared with.

**A new answer to your question.** When an administrator has the AI
Assistant answer one of your questions again, you are notified in Odoo
(*Notifications* in the messaging menu) or by email. On a record the new
answer is a note in reply to your question, opening with *New answer to the
question of*. In the app it shows in the conversation, opening with *New
answer to your question of* and the question; *Open it in the AI
Assistant*, the mail's *View* button and the notification open the app on
that conversation. Rate it as any
answer.

**Review (administrators).** *Review > To Review* lists the answers that
got a thumbs-down, were not covered or were not answered, until they are
closed. Each is a candidate for a README fix, since ``module_docs`` serves
the READMEs as deployed. Open one to read its question, answer, attempts
and cost, with a link to its AI conversation. Click *Fixed* once the README
or the code is corrected, or *Dismiss* when there is nothing to correct;
both also work on a selection in the list. Once the fix is deployed, *Send
new answer* asks the AI Assistant the question again as its asker, who is
notified of the new answer; the answer is marked *Fixed* at the same time
if it is still to review. The weekly digest mail lists every answer still
to review, those of the last 7 days marked *New*, each with a *Review*
link to it. *Review > Assistant Answers* holds every question (filters *Not
helpful*, *Not covered*, *Unavailable*, *Pending*, *To Review*, *Fixed*,
*Dismissed*); *Review > Tool Calls* (filters *Denied*, *Errors*) shows what
each answer looked up. *Configuration > Settings* sets the AI Assistant.

Known issues / Roadmap
======================

* Claude only: when no Claude model answers (an outage, a missing key, the
  workspace's spend limit), the AI Assistant does not answer. There is no
  fallback to another model and no per-employee budget in Odoo, so one heavy user
  can spend the workspace's monthly limit for everyone.
* It reads and never acts: it changes no record and offers no actions.
* An Ask AI answer is seen by everyone who can see the record. Text written
  into a record or an incoming email can steer an answer, but cannot make it
  change anything.
* A shared AI report shows the owner's data, which may be wider than what
  its recipients may read themselves.
* An Ask AI question refused when its answer job runs (the asker left the
  *AI Assistant* group, or Ask AI is no longer available on the record) is
  closed as *Unavailable* without a note on the record: only the asker learns
  of it, from a notification shown while they have Odoo open.
* An AI report page holds no images, SVG, links or fonts of its own, and
  its charts are those of Odoo's Chart.js 4.
* Tracked changes are told apart by their field's label, as a message names
  them: a hidden field's change Odoo logs on another record shows there
  when that record's model tracks a readable field of the same label, or
  has no relational field to or from the hidden field's model; a readable
  field's change is left out where a hidden field of its model shares its
  label. With ``mail_tracking``, its tracking values are filtered by field.
* A bank account's number masks a message by the accounts its record links
  to when the tools run, as stored or sanitized: a mail sent before the
  record's account, or the account's number, changed keeps its text, and
  so does a number written in another form or on a record that does not
  link to its account.
* A URL token is recognised as a parameter whose name ends in ``token``,
  a portal link's ``hash`` beside its ``pid``, or the rest of a URL past a
  path of ``_url_token_paths``: a token under another parameter name or
  path reads as it is, and of several ``hash`` parameters around one
  ``pid`` only the nearest is redacted.
* In an HTML text, a character or tag ``html2plaintext`` drops inside a
  token's mark splits the mark (a ``\r``, also as a sanitized field stores
  ``&#13;``, a ``\f`` or ``\v``, ``&#0;``, ``&#1;``, a malformed ``&#``,
  ``</td>``, ``<!x>``): the text shown redacts the token, a filter still
  finds its value. Odoo writes no such link.
* A model that searches names in its own code refuses a name filter while
  any of its rows holds a text the tools show otherwise than stored, one
  the employee may not read included; otherwise that code matches the
  stored texts. Its fields are those ``NAME_SEARCH_FIELDS`` declares
  without a context key that widens the search: a product's supplier codes,
  searched with a partner in the context, are not among them. A filter on
  any other text field that is not stored is matched by the field's search
  method, though its value shows redacted.
* A guest's name is masked only in the display names Odoo builds from the
  guest record, by the fields it builds them from, not by their value: a
  group chat that has a name still reads *a name on the AI deny list* while
  a guest is among the members Odoo would name it by. Where Odoo copies a
  guest's name as text, the copy shows and a filter matches it: a live
  chat's name, the record name a message, rating or activity shows
  (``record_name``, ``res_name``), the live chat report and the messages
  live chat posts about the visitor. A live chat guest is *Visitor*
  (*Visitor #N* with ``website_livechat``) unless the visitor types a name.
* A display name shows a denied field's value where it holds the value's
  string form in any case: a value Odoo writes into the name otherwise, as a
  selection's label or a date or an amount in the employee's language, is
  not found, and the name shows as Odoo builds it.
* A display name is checked for the denied values of its own record and of
  the records it is built from by a link. A text that copies such a value
  or name keeps it: a line's description, a contact's name, which holds
  its company's (a company named by its hidden email shows it in its
  contacts' names), the record name a message, rating or activity shows
  (``record_name``, ``res_name``), a message's ``email_from`` and the old
  and new values of a tracked link in a record's history.
* A like filter on a text matches the rows with each ``_``, vertical tab
  and whole placeholder of its value read as any run of characters, and
  checks each row so matched for the marks of a token (``token=``,
  ``pid=``, a path of
  ``_url_token_paths``); it reads every row, or those a trigram index of
  the text finds, for that value with tags between its characters, a
  non-ASCII character of an ilike value as any run of characters too. Then
  the filter takes the tokens' values out of the rows that hold one in
  SQL, taking the inline tags out of a text
  wherever a run of URL characters after ``?``, ``&``, ``;``, ``#``,
  ``%``, ``/`` or ``=`` reaches an inline tag, or a ``<`` that one
  follows: in a URL, as a link's visible URL does, and in any other text
  as well, as ``Hello&nbsp;<b>``, ``50%</b>`` or ``and/or<b>``.
* A text filter is slower than Odoo's own. Over 10 KB HTML texts, a word
  took 0.04 ms a row against 0.01 ms for Odoo's own filter; a wildcard
  took 0.13 ms a row that holds a token and 1.0 ms one whose tags it takes
  out, a URL's or such a text's alike. A short or common like value is
  about 10 times slower than Odoo's own, as the token check runs on every
  row it matches. The index finds the rows of a value across tags less
  closely than those of the value: on a table of about 200,000 rows with a
  trigram index, a like takes several to a few tens of milliseconds where
  Odoo's own takes under 5. On a translated text the like uses no trigram
  index, as its condition reads the language's value: it scans the table.
* A ``=like`` or ``=ilike`` filter on a text that may hold a URL token
  uses no btree index of the text, which can serve Odoo's own ``=like``
  for a value that starts with a fixed text: only a trigram index serves
  its regex across tags. Nor does ``=``, ``!=``, ``in`` or ``not in``
  with a value that holds the placeholder, matched as a ``=like`` of it.
* A vertical tab typed in an ``=`` or ``in`` value without the
  placeholder is compared with the stored text, where a like reads it as
  a token's place. A text that holds the placeholder outside a token, as
  a quoted answer does, is found by no ``=`` with the text as shown, which
  the filter reads with a token's place for each placeholder. A vertical
  tab a text holds reads as a token's place too: the placeholder of a
  filter's value meets it.
* A like value, or an equality value that holds the placeholder, of over
  5,000 characters as a like reads them (a run of wildcards, vertical
  tabs and placeholders, and for an ilike of non-ASCII characters, counts
  once) on a
  text that may hold a URL token is refused:
  PostgreSQL finds a regex across tags of many thousand characters too
  complex.
* With PostgreSQL's ``unaccent``, which turns ``«``, ``»``, ``‹``, ``›``
  and a few other signs into ``<`` and ``>``, a like or not like value is
  not found across a tag that holds one of them: in such a row it is
  compared with the stored text, a URL token in it included.
* A search of messages that names no model, or more than 5, looks at the
  first 10,000 matches only, the newest first unless it sorts otherwise;
  link previews are shown for the newest 10,000 messages with a link only.
* Two concurrent presses of *Send new answer* on one question queue one new
  answer, guarded by a row lock on the question and a check read past the
  transaction's snapshot. The tests run in one transaction and do not
  exercise that race.
* ``find_models`` and ``find_menus`` match a word only where a word starts:
  *keeping* does not find *Timekeeping*.
* ``find_models`` and ``find_menus`` do not reduce a word of three letters:
  *OTs* does not find *OT*.
* ``find_models`` and ``find_menus`` form singulars by spelling alone:
  *cookies* does not find *Cookie*, nor *statuses* or *analyses* *Status* or
  *Analysis*.
* ``find_models`` never matches the fields every model has by label, also
  where a model labels one otherwise, such as ``create_date`` as *Creation
  Date*.
* ``do`` is a common word ``find_models`` and ``find_menus`` leave out, also
  where it stands for a delivery order.
