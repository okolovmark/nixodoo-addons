Project Team Dashboard

A team’s work in Project at a glance: a Team Dashboard of workload cards per member, readable as records too (project.team.board), a multi-project Gantt chart with dependency lags, baselines and the stage history of tasks, a Backlog list, planned dates of a task that roll up from its sub-tasks, and, in an AI-driven project, a Deadline that follows the planned end. It builds on project_ai_driven (task type, key, size, modules of a task, stage log) and on hr_timesheet (logged hours, employees).

Stages (project.task.type) gain two flags: In Progress Stage (tasks there are being worked on; the dashboard picks a member’s current task from them) and Backlog Stage (tasks there form the backlog).

What counts

  • A task is closed when its state is Done or Cancelled, as in Odoo; a folded stage closes nothing. The cards, the Backlog and the baselines take open tasks only, and so does the Gantt chart unless Show Closed is on.
  • Task templates and the sub-tasks under them are left out everywhere. Project templates are not offered in the project pickers, and the tasks of project templates and tasks without a project are left out of the Manager and Department modes and of the Backlog.
  • Every day a user sees is a day in the viewer’s time zone (UTC when none is set): today, what is overdue, the dates on the cards, in the Gantt chart and in the Excel export, and the day a deadline-only task is drawn on. A member’s hours logged Today and their estimated completion count from the member’s own day, the one Odoo dates their timesheets on: their time zone, else UTC.

Planned dates and sub-tasks

A task has a planned start and a planned end (planned_date_start, planned_date_end, date and time), shown as Planned Dates before the Deadline on the task form and, hidden by default, in the task list. A task without sub-tasks keeps the planned dates it is given, and none until it is given some: assigning or closing the task sets none of them. An end before the start is refused. A task created from a template gets no planned dates.

A task with active sub-tasks takes its planned start and end from them: the earliest and the latest planned date of those sub-tasks, or no dates while none of them is dated. Archived sub-tasks are ignored, and the roll-up climbs every level (Milestone > Task > Sub-task). Such a task is flagged is_planned_from_subtasks: its planned dates are read-only on the task form and list, and a write that changes them is refused with a message to change the sub-tasks instead.

Deadline

In a project flagged AI-driven Project the Deadline of a task becomes its planned end, date and time, a rolled-up one included, when the planned end or the project differs from the plan the Deadline last followed. Each task records that plan in date_deadline_planned_end and date_deadline_project_id; a Deadline written by hand records the current plan, so it stays until the plan changes. Elsewhere, and on a task without a planned end, the Deadline stays as it is. The task form and its Sub-tasks and Blocked By lines carry the plan, so the Deadline follows the planned end as it is edited there. Installing or updating the module fills the plan of each task with its current planned end and project, and changes no Deadline.

Backlog

Project > Backlog lists the open tasks in a Backlog stage with Key, Task, Task Type, Priority Level, Size, Assignees, Deadline (the planned end), Estimate (h) and Tags; Project, Stage, Start (the planned start) and Modules are optional columns. A row is red once its planned end is past and grey while the task is Waiting. Filters My Tasks, Unassigned, Blocked (Waiting) and Overdue (planned end before today), groupings by Assignee, Start Date, Project and Type. Project users also search it by Module, a module’s technical name or title, as in the task search of project_ai_driven. It opens filtered on unassigned tasks and on the user’s Default Dashboard Project, when that project is not archived and the user reads it in the selected companies. Estimate (h) is the task’s Estimate (allocated_hours), and Size the size project_ai_driven computes from it.

Team Dashboard

Project > Team Dashboard is a client action with three modes:

  • Project: one card per user assigned to an open task of the project, plus an Unassigned card for its open tasks nobody is assigned to. Only this mode offers the Gantt chart.
  • Manager: cards for the users below a manager (the viewer by default) in the employee hierarchy, down to a chosen depth (1 to 5 levels or all), across every project.
  • Department: cards for the users of a department (the viewer’s by default), across every project.

In the Manager and Department modes only members with an open task get a card, and each card lists the projects of the member’s tasks.

Each card shows the member’s current task (the most recently updated task in an In Progress stage), the queue of other in-progress tasks, the backlog, other open tasks, the open task count, estimated and remaining hours, hours logged today and in the selected period (This Week, Last Week, This Month, Last Month or a custom range; a week starts on the first day of the week of the viewer’s language), and an estimated completion date (ETA). The hours logged are the member’s timesheets on the project in Project mode, and their timesheets of the selected companies in the Manager and Department modes. The ETA plans the remaining hours on the working schedule of the member’s employee (the company’s when the employee has none) with leaves taken into account, and falls back to 5.7 hours a day, every day, when there is no employee or no schedule. The date of a task on a card is its planned end, labelled Deadline, red with (OVERDUE) once that day is past. In Project mode buttons open the project’s form, its unassigned backlog, its tasks and the viewer’s tasks in it; a card opens the member’s backlog and kanban (Project mode) and closed tasks.

The dashboard keeps its mode, team, period and view, and the Gantt chart its settings and the window it shows, across a switch between cards and chart and a return through the breadcrumb.

Users carry a Default Dashboard Project, editable from their preferences; the dashboard and the Backlog open on it while the user finds it among the active projects of the selected companies, the dashboard otherwise on the first project found.

The Team Dashboard as a record

A reader that can only search and read records, such as an agent behind an Odoo connector, gets the numbers of the screen from project.team.board (Team Dashboard board). It stores nothing: its rows are an SQL view of one row per active project that is not a project template, per manager (an active user whose employee manages another active employee) and per active department, each in three periods, This Week, Last Week and This Month. A row has:

  • name: the project, manager or department and the period, e.g. “Website · This Week”; a department by its own name, not its complete name;
  • mode: project, manager or department;
  • project_id, manager_user_id or department_id: the row’s subject, set for its mode only;
  • period: this_week, last_week or this_month;
  • data: exactly what the screen shows for that subject and period, computed when it is read and never stored. It is the result of the screen’s own call (get_team_dashboard_data, get_team_dashboard_by_manager with all levels, or get_team_dashboard_by_department) for the period’s first and last day: the screen’s week, seven days from the first day of the week of the reader’s language (the context language), and the month from its first to its last day, on the reader’s today in their time zone. The member cards, today’s and the period’s logged hours and the estimated completion are in it, with every day counted as on the screen.

A user finds and reads only the rows they open on the screen; the others are left out of searches, counts, groups and name searches, and reading one by its id raises an access error. A project row follows the project’s own access (its visibility and team members, or its members where project_member_access is installed); a manager row is the user’s own or that of a manager below one of their employees; a department row is that of the user’s department or one they manage. Timesheet approvers, every project manager among them, and HR officers find every manager and department. Either way a manager or a department of a company the user has not selected stays hidden, the user’s own manager row aside, and the screen does not open it either. A user who may not read timesheets finds no project row, as the screen refuses them a project too. Reading every field of a project computes no dashboard, as the board is a model of its own, and a read of the board naming no fields lists its rows without data. The design record is docs/adr/0016-the-team-dashboard-is-a-record-of-a-sql-view-with-its-data-computed-on-reading.md in this repository.

Gantt Chart

The Gantt Chart plots, for the selected projects the user can read, the tasks that have planned dates or a deadline, with their ancestors, on a timeline: Day, Week or Month scale paged a month at a time (a week on Day, a quarter on Month), Fit All and Today, grouped by user (the default), by task or by stage, Show Closed to add the Done and Cancelled tasks, faceted search (Assignee, Task, Key, Stage, Type) with a jump to any shown task by key or name, several projects at once. A task with a single planned date gets a one-day bar, and its tooltip shows that date alone. Bars take the colour of their stage (grey for a stage whose tasks shown are all closed, legend built from the stages shown) and show the task’s progress; an open task whose bar ends before today gets a red outline. Grouped by user, the bars of one person that overlap are highlighted, a task and its own sub-tasks excepted. Dependencies are drawn as orange elbow connectors from the predecessor’s end into the successor’s start. Export Excel downloads the open tasks of those projects that have planned dates or a deadline as an .xlsx Gantt chart, sorted by start, with today’s column marked and the day headers in the user’s language (“05 mars” in French); a planned date the task does not have leaves its Start or End cell empty. The file is named after the first of those projects. The search facets and grouping are not applied. The chart is drawn with vis-timeline 7.7.0, which the module ships (static/lib/vis-timeline, MIT License) and loads on the first chart shown; the library injects its own styles.

  • Attribute columns: the left pane can show Assignees, Deadline, Estimate, Spent hours and Stage next to the task name, as a fixed-width grid with a header row; hours are right-aligned, and the Deadline is red when past on an open task. The choice is stored per user (res.users.gantt_columns); the first four are on by default. Columns are shown only while their total width plus 140 px for the task name fits in 60% of the chart width (all five need a chart about 1010 px wide); otherwise they are hidden. The pane re-measures after a window resize or a column change. A cell tooltip reads “<Column>: <value>”; a header tooltip explains the column. The column that repeats the grouping is left out: Assignees in Group by User, Stage in Group by Stage; its checkbox is greyed out there but keeps the stored choice.
  • Moving bars: a bar moves and resizes by whole days and keeps the time of day of its dates. A move writes only the planned dates the task has; a resize that drags the edge of a missing date sets it. The bars of deadline-only tasks, of tasks planned from their sub-tasks and of tasks the user cannot write do not move.
  • Dependencies: dragging the link handle, shown just past the end of a hovered or selected bar and wider on a touch screen, onto another bar makes the second task depend on the first (core depend_on_ids). Both tasks need a project with Task Dependencies on. Clicking an arrow opens a popover to set its dependency lag or remove the link.
  • Dependency lag (project.task.dependency.lag): calendar days per dependency, zero or more; no row means lag 0. The chart only checks it, on the viewer’s days: a link whose successor starts before the predecessor’s end plus the lag is drawn red with a warning. No date moves automatically. Copying a task keeps the lags of the dependencies that are copied with it; removing a dependency removes its lag.
  • Baseline (project.baseline / project.baseline.line): Save Baseline (Project Administrators) stores the planned start, planned end and deadline of every open task with at least one of these dates, one baseline per project shown. The new baseline becomes the project’s Active Baseline (baseline_id) and replaces the previous one. Show Baseline draws it as grey bars under the tasks still in that project, and the tooltip shows the shift of the planned end, e.g. “+3 d vs baseline”. Turning Show Baseline on when no task shown has a baseline raises a warning pointing to Save Baseline.
  • Sub-tasks: grouped by task, user or stage, a sub-task row is nested under its parent’s row when both fall in the same group. Otherwise the sub-task is a top-level row labelled “sub-task of <parent key>” (its name when it has no key), also when the user cannot read the parent, or reads it as a follower in a project they cannot read: the label is all the chart shows of it.
  • Unscheduled (N): open tasks with no planned dates and no deadline are listed in a collapsed group at the end of the chart, where a click on a name opens the task, and counted in a tasks without dates button that opens them as a list.
  • Milestones: Milestone-type tasks and tasks with only a deadline are drawn as a diamond on their date (the planned start, the planned end of a task that has no start, or the deadline), and exported as milestones on that date in the Excel Gantt chart. A Milestone shown with its sub-task rows and spanning more than one date is drawn as a bar.
  • Keyboard, languages and colours: the open icon of a task row and the names under Unscheduled (N) are buttons reached with Tab and pressed with Enter or Space; toolbar icons are hidden from screen readers, which read the buttons’ names. The Week scale starts each week on the first day of the week of the viewer’s language, and the time axis names the months and weekdays in it. In a right-to-left language the chart is mirrored: row labels, columns and their header on the right, time running leftwards, progress filling from the right, arrows from a bar’s left end; a row label too long for the sidebar is cut in its name, the task key kept whole. Outlines, highlights, the link handle and the chart’s text and lines take the web client’s colour variables.
  • Stage History (off by default, in every grouping): each task row expands into one row per stage visit, in the stage’s colour and chained by orange connectors. The visits come from the stage log of project_ai_driven (project.task.stage.log), which holds every change of a task’s stage, with or without a line in the chatter, and are shown for the tasks the user can see. A row with a stage starts a visit at its date and ends the visit before it; a row with no stage only ends the visit before it. The history starts at the task’s first row. The visit a task is still in runs to now; for a Done or Cancelled task it ends when its state or stage last changed, never before it starts. A deleted stage keeps its name and shares the colour and the legend entry of tasks without a stage. A visit shorter than a day is drawn one day wide; history rows are read-only. The task row then shows a read-only summary bar from the start of the first visit to the end of the last one, in the current stage’s colour (the planned dates stay in its tooltip), and the overlap highlighting of the User grouping is off. A task with no row in the log keeps its own bar.

Project users reach lags and baselines only through the Gantt methods, which check their rights on the tasks and projects first. The server methods of the dashboard and the chart (get_team_dashboard_data, get_team_dashboard_by_manager, get_team_dashboard_by_department, get_gantt_chart_data) answer with the keys of the 16.0 branch: planned_hours carries the allocated hours, kanban_state the task’s state, and progress is a percentage. readme/gantt_data_model.puml draws the models of the Gantt chart; the design record is docs/adr/0015-project-team-dashboard-on-20-0-follows-the-odoo-20-core-where-it-differs-from-16-0.md in this repository.

Installation

Installing the module needs nothing else. It adds the planned start and end columns to the task, empty, and computes nothing task by task: no task has a planned date yet, so no parent has one to take from its sub-tasks. Columns that exist already keep their values. The two columns of the plan a Deadline follows are filled with each task’s current planned end and project, so neither an install nor an update changes a Deadline.

Configuration

Projects

  • Tick Task Dependencies on the project to link its tasks from the Gantt chart.
  • On the project’s task stages (Project > Configuration > Task Stages, developer mode only) tick In Progress Stage on the working stages and Backlog Stage on the backlog stages; the Backlog list and the Team Dashboard read these flags. Folding a stage does not close its tasks: their state does.
  • Tick AI-driven Project on a project for the Deadline of its tasks to follow their planned end (see Usage). Ticking it changes no Deadline, and neither does installing or updating the module: each task follows from the next change of its planned end or its project.

Users

  • Each user sets their Default Dashboard Project in Preferences (an administrator on the user form); the Backlog menu and the Team Dashboard open on it.
  • The estimated completion of a member card plans on the working schedule of the user’s employee in the current company, or of the company: give each member an employee with a schedule for an estimate that skips weekends, leaves and public holidays. A member’s Today and estimated completion start from the member’s day, the one Odoo dates their timesheets on: in the time zone of their preferences, else in UTC.

Access

Team Dashboard tasks are read with the user’s project rights, and so are the logged hours in Project mode. In the Manager and Department modes the logged hours of the listed users (their timesheets of the selected companies, across all projects), the employee hierarchy and the department members are read with elevated rights; the department picker list and the viewer’s own department are loaded with the user’s own rights. In every mode the member’s employee and working schedule behind the estimated completion are read with elevated rights, and so are the name of the project and the key of the parent of each task on a card.

Who opens which team in those two modes follows the user’s employee:

  • Manager: the user themselves, or a manager one of whose employees reports to one of the user’s active employees, directly or further down the hierarchy (the employee’s Manager, through archived employees too), across companies.
  • Department: a department the user’s employee belongs to, or one it is the Manager of.
  • A user with Timesheets: User: all timesheets (Timesheets Administrator includes it) or Employees: Officer: Manage all employees opens every manager and every department. So does every Project / Administrator: Odoo gives project administrators the first of these rights.
  • In every case a department opens only without a company or in one the user has selected, and a manager other than the user only with an active employee in a company the user has selected; the manager’s team itself may reach into other companies.
  • The user’s archived employees open no team, and the cards of every mode leave out archived projects, employees and tasks, also for a call that asks for archived records.

The Team Dashboard board (project.team.board) follows the same rules: every internal user reads it, and finds the rows of the teams and projects they open on the screen.

A manager’s team is everyone below their employees, the manager left out, also where the hierarchy loops back: Odoo lets an employee manage their own manager, and the Manager of a department who belongs to it gets themselves as Manager.

The pickers still list every user and department. Any other choice is refused: the user gets an error message, and the picker goes back to the manager or department shown before, whose cards stay on screen. A user who opens every manager and department reads why in the dashboard instead: “No employee record found for this user” for a manager with no active employee; for one whose employees are all in companies they have not selected, the same “in the selected companies” when one of those companies is one they may select, else the plain message. A department of a company they may select but have not selected gets “Department not in the selected companies”; one of a company they may not select, or one that no longer exists, “Department not found”. Any other user reads the same of their own department, or one their employee manages, in a company they have not selected. Give a team lead an employee with the right Manager for them to see their team.

Gantt chart: dependencies can be linked and lags edited by users with write access to the dependent task and read access to the other one. Only Project / Administrator can save a baseline, and needs write access to the project. Project Administrators read and manage the baselines of the projects of their companies, or without a company, and read the dependency lags of the tasks they can read; project users reach them only through the Gantt chart. These rights are ir.access rows (security/ir.access.csv).

Usage

Plan a task

  • The Planned Dates of a task are above its deadline on the task form; show them in the task list from its optional columns.
  • A task with active sub-tasks shows its planned dates read-only: plan its sub-tasks and the task follows them.
  • The next occurrence of a recurring task, and each of its sub-tasks, gets the planned dates of the task it repeats moved by the recurrence interval, as Odoo moves the Deadline. When a move onto a shorter month would leave the start at or after the end, the end stays and the start keeps the length of the task before it.
  • In an AI-driven project the Deadline follows the planned end: when a task gets a planned end or its planned end really changes, by hand, in the Gantt chart or from its sub-tasks, its Deadline becomes that end, date and time. A task moved into an AI-driven project with a planned end gets that Deadline too, and so does a copy of a task. Nothing else moves the Deadline: not a stage change, not a start moved alone in the Gantt chart, not a change to a sub-task that leaves its parent’s planned end where it was, not the same planned end saved again. A Deadline you set by hand, alone or together with the planned end or the project, stays until the planned end changes again or the task moves into an AI-driven project; so does the Deadline Odoo gives the next occurrence of a recurring task. On the task form the Deadline changes as you edit the planned end; a Deadline you type after it is the one saved. Changing the Deadline alone leaves the plan where it is, and clearing the planned end leaves the Deadline. In other projects the planned end does not change the Deadline.

Work the backlog

Project > Backlog opens the open Backlog-stage tasks of your Default Dashboard Project, filtered on Unassigned; remove that filter to see assigned tasks too. A Default Dashboard Project that is archived, of a company you have not selected, or one you cannot read, gives no project filter: the list opens on every project. Reorder by drag, filter My Tasks, Blocked or Overdue, and group by Assignee, Start Date, Project or Type. To assign a task, open it.

Team Dashboard

Project > Team Dashboard:

  1. Pick the mode: Project (type a project name), Manager (pick the manager and the Depth) or Department. Manager and Department open only the teams your rights allow (see Configuration).
  2. Pick the Period for the logged-hours figures, or Custom Range with start and end dates. A week starts on the first day of the week of your language (Monday in French, Sunday in English (US)).
  3. Read each member card: current task, queue, backlog, estimated and remaining hours, hours logged, estimated completion. Click a task to open it, or the card’s Backlog, Kanban or Closed buttons for the member’s tasks.
  4. In Project mode, switch to the Gantt Chart with the chart button next to the cards button. Change the Scale (Day, Week, Month; the Week scale starts each week on your language’s first day), page earlier or later with the arrows around Today, press Fit All to see every task, Group by user (the default), task or stage, press Show Closed to include Done and Cancelled tasks, add more projects with Add project…, and press Export Excel for an .xlsx (the export always leaves out closed tasks).

Opening a task and coming back through the breadcrumb, or switching between the cards and the chart, keeps the mode, team, period and the chart as you left them.

Read the Team Dashboard as a record

Pick a row of project.team.board by its mode, subject and period, and name data among the fields you read:

search_read('project.team.board',
            [('mode', '=', 'project'), ('project_id', '=', ID),
             ('period', '=', 'this_week')],
            ['data'])

For a manager use ('mode', '=', 'manager') and ('manager_user_id', '=', USER_ID), for a department ('mode', '=', 'department') and ('department_id', '=', DEPARTMENT_ID); the periods are this_week, last_week and this_month. data holds the dict the screen draws: members, one card each (current task, queue, backlog, other tasks, hours, hours_logged_today, hours_logged_period, estimated_completion), and period_start and period_end. Each row read with data costs one dashboard calculation, so read the rows you need. A read without a field list leaves data out: it lists the rows you may open, and data comes only when you name it.

Plan in the Gantt chart

  • Columns: open the columns menu and tick the attribute columns to show; hover a column name there or in the header for what it holds. On a narrow screen the columns are hidden until the window is wide enough for them, or until fewer are ticked. Grouped by User or Stage, the column with the same information (Assignees or Stage) is not shown.
  • Find a task: type its key or part of its name in the search box and pick it under Go to task (or press Enter for the first one); the chart scrolls to its bar, selects it and briefly highlights its row. Pick a field there (Assignee, Task, Key, Stage, Type) to narrow the chart to one of its values.
  • Link tasks: hover the predecessor’s bar, or select it (tap it on a touch screen), and drag the handle shown just past its end (right of the bar, left of it in a right-to-left language) onto the dependent task. Click the arrow to enter Lag, days and press Save, or press Remove to delete the dependency. A red arrow means the dependent task starts too early; move its dates yourself.
  • Open a task with the open icon before its name. From the keyboard, Tab to that icon, or to a task name under Unscheduled (N), and press Enter or Space.
  • Project Administrators press Save Baseline to store the current plan; it replaces the project’s previous baseline. Press Show Baseline to compare the plan against it: the baseline is drawn as a thin bar under each task bar. When none of the tasks shown has a baseline yet, a warning says so and points to Save Baseline.
  • Move or resize a bar to replan a task by whole days. A move changes only the planned dates the task has; drag the padded edge of a one-day bar to give the task its missing date. The bar of a task planned from its sub-tasks does not move: drag its sub-tasks.
  • Press Stage History to see under each task the stages it went through and how long it stayed in each. Every stage change the stage log holds counts, whoever made it: a move by a script or an assistant that left no line in the chatter shows too. The log starts when project_ai_driven is installed: a task older than that whose stage has not changed since keeps its own bar.
  • Expand Unscheduled (N), or press the tasks without dates button, to find tasks without any date and open them to plan them.
  • In a right-to-left language the chart is mirrored: task names and columns on the right, time running leftwards, arrows from a bar’s left end into the next bar’s right end.

Known issues / Roadmap

  • The Gantt chart does not reschedule tasks: a violated dependency lag is only drawn red.
  • Dependency lags count calendar days, not working days.
  • Each project keeps a single baseline; saving a new one deletes the previous one, so there is no baseline history.
  • The Gantt attribute columns cannot be sorted.
  • The module ships no translations yet: it has no i18n folder, so its own texts and messages are not translated into the user’s language.
