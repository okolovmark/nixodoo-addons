Project Team Dashboard

A team’s work in Project at a glance: a Team Dashboard of workload cards per member, a multi-project Gantt chart with dependency lags and baselines, a Backlog list, and planned dates of a task that roll up from its sub-tasks. It builds on project_ai_driven (task type, estimate, size, modules of a task, stage log) and on hr_timesheet (logged hours, employees).

Stages (project.task.type) gain two flags: In Progress Stage (tasks there are being worked on; the dashboard picks a member’s current task from them) and Backlog Stage (tasks there form the backlog).

Planned dates and sub-tasks

A task has a planned start and a planned end (planned_date_start, planned_date_end), on the task form and, hidden by default, in the task list. A task without sub-tasks keeps the planned dates it is given, and none until it is given some: assigning or closing the task sets none of them. An end before the start is refused. These are the fields of the OCA module project_timeline, which this module does not need. With it installed, its computations are replaced by these, so its rules that take the assignment date as planned start and the closing date as planned end do not run (project_team_dashboard_timeline keeps its fields on the form and list).

A task with active sub-tasks takes its planned start and end from them: the earliest and the latest planned date of those sub-tasks, or no dates while none of them is dated. Archived sub-tasks are ignored, and the roll-up climbs every level (Milestone > Task > Sub-task). In an AI-driven project the Deadline follows a rolled-up planned end as it follows one entered by hand (see Usage); elsewhere the roll-up leaves the Deadline alone. Such a task is flagged is_planned_from_subtasks: its planned dates are read-only on the task form and list, and a write that changes them is refused with a message to change the sub-tasks instead. A save of a recurring task for This and following tasks or All tasks does the same on every task of the recurrence it writes: it is refused when it changes the planned dates of one planned from its sub-tasks, and the message names that task; a save that changes sub-tasks rolls up the planned dates of each of them again.

Backlog

Project > Backlog lists the tasks in a Backlog stage with Key, Task, Assignee, Type, Priority, Size and Estimate (h), filters My Tasks, Unassigned, Blocked, Overdue and groupings by Assignee, Start Date, Project and Type. Project users also search it by Module, a module’s technical name or title, as in the task search of project_ai_driven. It opens filtered on the user’s Default Dashboard Project and on unassigned tasks. Estimate (h) is the task’s Estimate in hours, and Size the size project_ai_driven computes from it.

Team Dashboard

Project > Team Dashboard is a client action with three modes:

  • View by Project: one card per user assigned to an open task of the project (plus an Unassigned card).
  • View by Manager: cards for the subordinates of a user, taken from the employee hierarchy down to a chosen depth (1 to 5 levels or all), across every project.
  • View by Department: cards for the users of a department.

Each card shows the member’s current task (the most recently updated task in an In Progress stage), the queue of other in-progress tasks, the backlog, other open tasks, open task count, estimated and remaining hours, hours logged today and in the selected period (This Week, Last Week, This Month, Last Month or a custom range), and an estimated completion date. The estimate plans the remaining hours on the employee’s working schedule with leaves taken into account, and falls back to 5.7 hours a day when no employee or schedule exists. Links open the member’s kanban, backlog or closed tasks.

Logged hours are timesheet lines: in View by Project the lines of the project, as far as the user may read them; in the other two modes the member’s lines on any project of the companies selected in the company switcher. Analytic lines without a project, such as those of invoices and bills, never count.

The screen works in the companies selected in the company switcher: every call it makes carries them, the Gantt chart and its edits included. An error answer, such as a team not found, shows under the toolbar, which stays for another pick.

Users carry a Default Dashboard Project, editable from their preferences.

The Team Dashboard as a record

A reader that can only search and read records, such as an agent behind an Odoo connector, gets the numbers of the screen from project.team.board (Team Dashboard board). It stores nothing: its rows are an SQL view of one row per active project, per manager (an active user whose employee manages another active employee) and per active department, each in three periods, This Week, Last Week and This Month. A row has:

  • name: the project, manager or department and the period, e.g. “Website · This Week”;
  • mode: project, manager or department;
  • project_id, manager_user_id or department_id: the row’s subject, set for its mode only;
  • period: this_week, last_week or this_month;
  • data: exactly what the screen shows for that subject and period, computed when it is read and never stored. It is the result of the screen’s own call (get_team_dashboard_data, get_team_dashboard_by_manager with all levels, or get_team_dashboard_by_department) for the period’s first and last day, weeks from Monday to Sunday and the month from its first to its last day, on the reader’s today in their time zone. The member cards, today’s and the period’s logged hours and the estimated completion (planned on the employee’s working schedule with leaves and public holidays) are in it. Only the period follows the reader’s time zone: today’s logged hours, the overdue tasks and the day the estimated completion starts from count on the server’s date, as on the screen.

A user finds and reads only the rows they open on the screen; the others are left out of searches, counts and groups, and reading one by its id raises an access error. A project row follows the project’s own access (its visibility, or its members where project_member_access is installed); a manager row is the user’s own or that of a manager below one of their employees; a department row is that of the user’s department or one they manage. Timesheet approvers, every project manager among them, and HR officers find every manager and department. Either way a manager or a department of a company the user has not selected stays hidden, the user’s own manager row aside, and the screen does not open it either. A user who may not read timesheets finds no project row, as the screen refuses them a project too. Reading every field of a project computes no dashboard, as the board is a model of its own, and a read of the board naming no fields lists its rows without data. The design record is docs/adr/0016-the-team-dashboard-is-a-record-of-a-sql-view-with-its-data-computed-on-reading.md in this repository.

Gantt Chart

The Gantt Chart view plots the planned dates of the tasks of the selected projects the user reads (a task they follow in a project they cannot read stays out) on a timeline: Day, Week or Month scale paged a month at a time (a week on Day, a quarter on Month), grouped by user (the default), by task or by stage, optional completed tasks, a counter of tasks without dates, faceted search with a jump to any shown task by key or name, several projects at once. Bars take the colour of their stage (closed stages grey, legend built from the stages shown); an open task past its planned end gets a red outline. Dependencies are drawn as orange elbow connectors from the predecessor’s end into the successor’s start. Export Excel downloads the open tasks of the selected projects that have planned dates or a deadline as an .xlsx Gantt chart named after the first of those projects the user reads, sorted by start date; closed tasks are always left out, and the search facets and grouping are not applied. The chart is drawn with the vis-timeline library, which the module ships (static/lib/vis-timeline, MIT License).

  • Attribute columns: the left pane can show Assignees, Deadline, Estimate, Spent hours and Stage next to the task name, as a fixed-width grid with a header row; hours are right-aligned. The choice is stored per user (res.users.gantt_columns); the first four are on by default. Columns are shown only while their total width plus 140 px for the task name fits in 60% of the chart width (all five need a chart about 1010 px wide); otherwise they are hidden. The pane re-measures after a window resize or a column change. A cell tooltip reads “<Column>: <value>”; a header tooltip explains the column. The column that repeats the grouping is left out: Assignees in Group by User, Stage in Group by Stage; its checkbox is greyed out there but keeps the stored choice.
  • Dependencies: dragging the handle of a task bar onto another bar makes the second task depend on the first (core depend_on_ids). Clicking an arrow opens a popover to set its dependency lag or remove the link.
  • Dependency lag (project.task.dependency.lag): calendar days per dependency, zero or more; no row means lag 0. The chart only checks it: a link whose successor starts before the predecessor’s end plus the lag is drawn red with a warning. No date moves automatically. Copying a task keeps the lags of the dependencies that are copied with it; removing a dependency removes its lag, on every task of a recurrence the save reaches.
  • Baseline (project.baseline / project.baseline.line): Save Baseline (Project Managers) stores the planned start, planned end and deadline of every open task with at least one of these dates, one baseline per project shown. The new baseline becomes the project’s Active Baseline (baseline_id) and replaces the previous one. Show Baseline draws it as grey bars under the tasks, and the tooltip shows the shift of the planned end, e.g. “+3 d vs baseline”. Turning Show Baseline on when no task shown has a baseline raises a warning pointing to Save Baseline.
  • Sub-tasks: grouped by task, user or stage, a sub-task row is nested under its parent’s row when both fall in the same group. Otherwise the sub-task is a top-level row labelled “sub-task of <parent key>” (its name when it has no key), also when the user cannot read the parent. A parent’s bar is read-only when its dates come from its sub-tasks; other bars move and resize by whole days. In Group by User a task and its own sub-tasks never count as conflicting.
  • Unscheduled (N): tasks with no planned dates and no deadline are listed in their own group at the end of the chart; a click opens the task.
  • Milestones: Milestone-type tasks and tasks with only a deadline are drawn as a diamond on their date, and exported as milestones in the Excel Gantt chart. A Milestone shown with its sub-task rows and spanning more than one date is drawn as a bar.
  • Stage History (off by default, in every grouping): each task row expands into one row per stage visit, in the stage’s colour and chained by orange connectors. The visits come from the stage log of project_ai_driven (project.task.stage.log, The stage log in that module), which holds every change of a task’s stage, with or without a line in the chatter, and are shown for the tasks the user can see. A row with a stage starts a visit at its date and ends the visit before it; a row with no stage only ends the visit before it. The history starts at the task’s first row. The visit a task is still in runs to now, or ends at its own start when its stage is closed (folded). A deleted stage keeps its name and shares the colour and the legend entry of tasks without a stage. A visit shorter than a day is drawn one day wide; history rows are read-only. The task row then shows a read-only summary bar from the start of the first visit to the end of the last one, in the current stage’s colour (the planned dates stay in its tooltip), and the User grouping’s conflict highlighting is off. A task with no row in the log keeps its own bar.

Lags and baselines are read and written only through the Gantt methods, which check the user’s rights on the tasks and projects first. readme/gantt_data_model.puml draws the models of the Gantt chart; the design record is docs/adr/0015-the-team-dashboard-needs-no-project-timeline-and-adopts-its-records-by-external-id.md in this repository.

Installation

A database that has none of this module’s records needs nothing beyond installing it. The install creates the planned date columns of the task when project_timeline has not created them, empty, and rolls the planned dates of every task with sub-tasks up from them; it logs Planned dates rolled up from sub-tasks on <n> tasks.

A database that holds these records under another module

The Backlog, the Team Dashboard and the Gantt chart may be installed already under another module, which then hands them over: that module drops them from its code and depends on this one, and its update installs this module first.

  • Update that module with the server stopped. Before any data file is loaded, the pre_init_hook takes the records that module holds under the external ids of this module (models, fields, views, actions, menus, access and record rules, the constraint of the dependency lag), with the constraints and foreign keys registered for them, so every record keeps its database id: baselines, dependency lags, the stage flags and the default dashboard project of every user stay. The module is the one that holds the external id of project.baseline.
  • The same hook hands the two views of that module that extend the planned dates of project_timeline to project_team_dashboard_timeline, which installs itself in the same update and loads its views over them. Put both modules on the addons path: without the glue module, those two views stay as they are and the form shows the planned dates twice.
  • A database where no other module holds the external id of project.baseline, project.baseline.line or project.task.dependency.lag is left untouched, even where a module holds records of the same names, and a second run finds nothing to move.

Configuration

Projects

  • Tick Task Dependencies on the project to link its tasks from the Gantt chart.
  • On the project’s task stages (Project > Configuration > Task Stages, developer mode only) tick In Progress Stage on the working stages and Backlog Stage on the backlog stages; the Backlog list and the Team Dashboard read these flags.
  • Tick AI-driven Project on a project for the Deadline of its tasks to follow their planned end (see Usage). Ticking it changes no Deadline: each task follows from the next change of its planned end. Installing the module, or updating it to 16.0.1.1.0, sets the Deadline of the open tasks (stage not folded, not archived) of AI-driven projects that have a planned end to the day of that end, without changing their last-updated date, in the time zone of the first active assignee who has a valid one, else of the user who last changed the task if still active, else UTC. Closed and archived tasks keep their Deadline until their planned end changes.

Users

  • Each user sets their Default Dashboard Project in Preferences (an administrator on the user form); the Backlog menu and the Team Dashboard open on it. When it is not among the projects the Team Dashboard lists (those the user reads in the companies selected in the switcher), the Team Dashboard opens on the first of them.
  • The estimated completion of a member card plans on the working schedule of the user’s employee in the current company, or of the company: give each member an employee with a schedule for an estimate that skips weekends, leaves and public holidays.

Access

Team Dashboard tasks are read with the user’s project rights, and so are the logged hours in View by Project mode. In the Manager and Department modes the logged hours of the listed users (their timesheets across all projects of the selected companies), the employee hierarchy and the department members are read with elevated rights; the department picker list and the viewer’s own department are loaded with the user’s own rights. In every mode the member’s employee and working schedule behind the estimated completion are read with elevated rights.

Who opens which team in those two modes follows the user’s employee:

  • View by Manager: the user themselves, or a manager one of whose employees reports to one of the user’s employees, directly or further down the hierarchy (the employee’s Manager), across companies.
  • View by Department: a department the user’s employee belongs to, or one it is the Manager of.
  • A user with Timesheets: User: all timesheets (Administrator includes it) or Employees: Officer opens every manager and every department. So does every Project / Administrator: Odoo gives project administrators the first of these rights.
  • In every case, whatever the company rules of employees and departments, a department opens only without a company or in one the user has selected, and a manager other than the user only with an active employee in a company the user has selected; the manager’s team itself may reach into other companies.
  • The user’s archived employees open no team, and the cards of every mode leave out archived projects, employees and tasks, also for a call that asks for archived records.

A manager’s team is everyone below their employees, the manager left out, also where the hierarchy loops back: Odoo lets an employee manage their own manager, and the Manager of a department who belongs to it gets themselves as Manager.

The manager picker lists every internal user. The department picker lists the departments the user finds: those of the companies they have selected or of none, where the departments’ company rule is active. Any other choice is refused with an error message, and the picker goes back to the manager or department shown before, whose cards stay on screen. A team refused when switching modes leaves the toolbar of that mode, whose picker opens another team.

A user who opens every manager and department is not refused but reads why under the toolbar:

  • “No employee record found for this user”: a manager without an active employee, or with active employees only in companies the user may not select.
  • “No employee record found for this user in the selected companies”: a manager whose active employees are all outside the selected companies, one of them in a company the user may select.
  • “Department not in the selected companies”: a department of a company the user may select but has not selected.
  • “Department not found”: a department of a company the user may not select, or one that no longer exists.

Any user opening their own team without an active employee reads the first message. Give a team lead an employee with the right Manager for them to see their team.

Gantt chart: dependencies can be linked and lags edited by users with write access to the dependent task. Only Project / Administrator can save a baseline, and needs write access to the project. Project managers can read dependency lags and manage baselines directly; project users reach them only through the Gantt chart.

Usage

Plan a task

  • The planned dates of a task are above its deadline on the task form; show them in the task list from its optional columns.
  • A task with active sub-tasks shows its planned dates read-only: plan its sub-tasks and the task follows them.
  • In an AI-driven project the Deadline follows the planned end: when a task gets a planned end or its planned end really changes, by hand, in the Gantt chart or from its sub-tasks, its Deadline becomes the day of that end in your time zone (UTC when your preferences have none). A task moved into an AI-driven project with a planned end gets that Deadline too. Nothing else moves the Deadline: not a stage change, not a start moved alone in the Gantt chart, not a change to a sub-task that leaves its parent’s planned end where it was, not the same planned end saved again. A Deadline you set by hand, alone or together with the planned end, stays until the planned end changes again, on every task of a recurrence the save reaches (This and following tasks, All tasks). On the task form the Deadline changes as you edit the planned end, and so does that of a task in its row of the Sub-tasks or Blocked By list where the list shows a planned end, which the stock lists do not. Changing the planned end back sets the Deadline to the day of that end again, not to a Deadline you had set by hand; a Deadline you type after the planned end is the one saved. Changing the Deadline alone leaves the plan where it is, and clearing the planned end leaves the Deadline. In other projects the planned end does not change the Deadline.

Work the backlog

Project > Backlog opens the Backlog-stage tasks of your Default Dashboard Project, filtered on Unassigned; remove that filter to see assigned tasks too. Reorder by drag, filter My Tasks, Blocked or Overdue, and group by Assignee, Start Date, Project or Type. To assign a task, open it.

Team Dashboard

Project > Team Dashboard shows the companies selected in the company switcher; select others there to see their projects, teams and hours.

  1. Pick the mode: View by Project (type a project name), View by Manager (pick the manager and the Depth) or View by Department.
  2. Pick the Period for the logged-hours figures, or Custom Range with start and end dates.
  3. Read each member card: current task, queue, backlog, estimated and remaining hours, hours logged, estimated completion. Click a task key to open it, or the card links to the member’s kanban, backlog or closed tasks.
  4. Switch to Gantt Chart to see planned dates. Change the Scale (Day, Week, Month), page earlier or later with the arrows around Today, Group by user (the default), task or stage, press Show Closed to include completed/closed tasks, add more projects with the Add project… search, and press Export Excel for an .xlsx (the export always leaves out closed tasks).

Read the Team Dashboard as a record

Pick a row of project.team.board by its mode, subject and period, and name data among the fields you read:

search_read('project.team.board',
            [('mode', '=', 'project'), ('project_id', '=', ID),
             ('period', '=', 'this_week')],
            ['data'])

For a manager use ('mode', '=', 'manager') and ('manager_user_id', '=', USER_ID), for a department ('mode', '=', 'department') and ('department_id', '=', DEPARTMENT_ID); the periods are this_week, last_week and this_month. data holds the dict the screen draws: members, one card each (current task, queue, backlog, other tasks, hours, hours_logged_today, hours_logged_period, estimated_completion), and period_start and period_end. The selected companies are those of the call’s allowed_company_ids, every company the reader may select when it names none: they decide the manager and department rows the reader finds and the hours of those rows. Each row read with data costs one dashboard calculation, so read the rows you need. A read without a field list leaves data out: it lists the rows you may open, and data comes only when you name it.

Plan in the Gantt chart

  • Columns: open the cog menu and tick the attribute columns to show; hover a column name there or in the header for what it holds. On a narrow screen the columns are hidden until the window is wide enough for them, or until fewer are ticked. Grouped by User or Stage, the column with the same information (Assignees or Stage) is not shown.
  • Find a task: type its key or part of its name in the search box and pick it under Go to task (or press Enter for the first one); the chart scrolls to its bar, selects it and briefly highlights its row.
  • Link tasks: hover the predecessor’s bar, drag the handle at its right end and drop it on the dependent task. Click the arrow to enter Lag, days and press Save, or press Remove to delete the dependency. A red arrow means the dependent task starts too early; move its dates yourself.
  • Project Managers press Save Baseline to store the current plan; it replaces the project’s previous baseline. Press Show Baseline to compare the plan against it: the baseline is drawn as a thin bar under each task bar. When none of the tasks shown has a baseline yet, a warning says so and points to Save Baseline.
  • Move or resize a bar to replan a task; it moves by whole days. The bar of a task planned from its sub-tasks does not move: drag its sub-tasks. A move changes only the planned dates the task has (a milestone’s start and end together). To set the missing date, drag the end of a bar that has only a start, or the start of one that has only an end.
  • Press Stage History to see under each task the stages it went through and how long it stayed in each. Every stage change counts, whoever made it: a move by a script or an assistant that left no line in the chatter shows too. A task older than the stage log with no stage change in its chatter shows its current stage alone, from its last stage update.
  • Expand Unscheduled (N) to find tasks without any date and open them to plan them.

Known issues / Roadmap

  • The Gantt chart does not reschedule tasks: a violated dependency lag is only drawn red.
  • Dependency lags count calendar days, not working days.
  • Each project keeps a single baseline; saving a new one deletes the previous one, so there is no baseline history.
  • The Gantt attribute columns cannot be sorted.
