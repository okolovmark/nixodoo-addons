# The Team Dashboard is a record of an SQL view, with its data computed on reading

Status: accepted (2026-11-19)

The 16.0 branch decided this first. Its record, `docs/adr/0016-the-team-dashboard-is-a-record-of-a-sql-view-with-its-data-computed-on-reading.md` there, holds the alternatives it weighed; this one states the decision as it stands on 20.0 and what differs.

The Team Dashboard is a client action: the screen calls `get_team_dashboard_data`, `get_team_dashboard_by_manager` or `get_team_dashboard_by_department` of `project.project` with a project, a manager or a department and two dates, and draws the dict it gets. A reader that only searches and reads records, such as an agent behind an Odoo connector, cannot call a method, and cannot redo the estimated completion, planned on the employee's working schedule with leaves and public holidays, from the records it reads.

**One record per dashboard.** From 20.0.1.3.0 `project_team_dashboard` has `project.team.board`, a model with no table of its own (`_auto = False`): an SQL view of one row per active project that is not a project template, per manager and per active department, each crossed with *This Week*, *Last Week* and *This Month*. A manager is an active user with an active employee that is the manager of another active employee, not one of their own. A row has `name` (the subject and the period), `mode`, `project_id`, `manager_user_id`, `department_id` and `period`, stored in the view, and `data`, a Json field computed when it is read and never stored. A department is named by its own name: Odoo 20 does not store its complete name. The id is `subject * 9 + mode * 3 + period + 1`, the same from one reading to the next while the subject exists; a period added later renumbers every row rather than giving two rows one id.

**The data is the screen's.** `data` is what the method of the row's mode returns for the row's subject and the period's first and last day: the module has one calculation. A week is the screen's default week (`project.project._get_dashboard_period`) of the reader's today, or of the day a week before for *Last Week*; the month runs from its first to its last day. Every day in the data is the reader's, as on the screen (ADR 0015): the period, today and the overdue tasks; a member's *Today* and estimated completion count from the member's own day. The compute runs in the reader's environment, its cache keyed on the user, the time zone, the companies and the language (`depends_context`), once per transaction, with the screen's `active_test` whatever the reader's: Odoo 20 keys a cache on `active_test` for relational fields only. It computes the managers and departments the user opens once for its rows, not once a row. No field of the project leads to it.

**A row is found by whoever opens it on the screen, and by nobody else.** Hidden means left out of `search`, `search_count`, `read_group` and `name_search`, and an access error for a `read` or `check_access` of its id; `has_access` and `_filtered_access` agree. Odoo 20 builds all of these from the model's access domain (`_access_domain`): `_search`, which `search_count`, `_read_group`, `name_search` and the read of a stored field go through, adds it to the query, and `has_access` filters ids by it. The model adds its condition to that domain, after the `ir.access` row that grants `base.group_user` the read. It is not an `ir.access` domain: Odoo caches those per user and companies until an access changes, while the managers and departments follow the employee hierarchy. The condition of a mode is a custom domain (`Domain.custom`), built when a search may return rows of the mode or a row of it is checked; its managers and departments are subqueries with the user's rights, so no list of every team is inlined for those who see them all, and with the compute's `active_test` whatever the reader's, on which the access cache does not key. The condition, per mode:

- every row of a mode is hidden from a user who lacks read access to a model the screen reads with their rights in that mode: projects and tasks in every mode, timesheets in the project mode, public employees in the manager mode and departments in the department mode;
- a project row follows the project, `('project_id', 'access', 'read')`: the project's own access domain decides, its visibility and team members, and `project_member_access` where it is installed;
- a manager row is one of `_get_openable_manager_users`, the set the screen's `_check_manager_mode_access` checks: the user, and every manager below one of the user's active employees, the walk passing through archived ones, or every manager for timesheet approvers (every project administrator among them), HR officers and the superuser. A manager other than the user counts only with an active employee in a company of the context. Odoo 20 gives public employees, which an internal user without HR rights searches, no company rule, so the company is a condition of the helper. The walk visits each employee once and searches `parent_id`: Odoo 20's `child_ids` holds active employees only;
- a department row is one of `_get_openable_departments`, the set `_check_department_mode_access` checks: among the departments of a company of the context or of none, those of the user's active employees and those one of them manages, or all of them for the same groups. The company is a condition of the helper, as for managers: a database may switch the departments' company rule off.

A user who opens no mode, for want of read access to projects, to tasks, or to the third model of each mode, gets the access error of the model, as Odoo 20 gives one for an empty access domain.

The view reads the tables of projects, users, partners, employees and departments, which the model names in `_depends`, so a search flushes the pending writes to them first.

**Consequences.**

- A read of `data` costs one dashboard calculation per row: a reader names the rows it needs. `read` and `search_read` without a field list leave `data` out, as for the task brief of ADR 0012.
- The board offers three periods and the screen's default depth for a manager (all levels); *Last Month*, a custom range and a depth stay on the screen.
- Two readers of one row may get different data, as on the screen: the tasks each finds, and the days of each one's time zone.
- The labels in `name` are in English, the source names.
- The screen and the board share the helpers: the manager and department modes refuse the teams of a company outside the context, and open the managers below an archived employee. Their hours are the timesheets (lines with a project) of the companies of the context, read as superuser. A user who sees every team is not refused a manager or a department: a manager with no active employee gets "No employee record found for this user", one with none in a company of the context the same "in the selected companies" when one of their employees is in a company the user may select (`res.users.company_ids`), else the plain message, a user id that does not exist "User not found"; a department outside the companies of the context "Department not in the selected companies" when its company is one the user may select, else "Department not found", as does an id that does not exist. Any other user is answered the same way, not refused, for their own department or one they manage outside the companies of the context: "Department not in the selected companies" when its company is one they may select, else "Department not found"; and for a manager below one of their employees with no active employee in a company of the context: "No employee record found for this user in the selected companies" when one of the manager's employees is in a company they may select, else the plain message. Every other manager and department is refused them, existing or not.
- A card names the project and keys the parent of every task its viewer reads, as Odoo names the record of a many-to-one, whoever may read it: a task in a project, or a sub-task of a task, the viewer cannot read no longer fails a mode.
- The estimated completion of a member reads their employee as superuser, as 16.0 does: a reader who may not read employees gets it on the employee's schedule and time zone. The *Today* of a member is the day core dates their timesheet lines on, in the time zone of their preferences, else UTC: the Odoo 20 web client sends the stored time zone only. Their estimated completion starts from their day in the time zone of their preferences, else their employee's, else UTC, as ADR 0015 sets out.
- `data` holds what the methods return. A change to them changes the board with the screen.

Reversal cost: drop the model, its access row and its view. Nothing is stored; a reader goes back to the screen. The helpers the board brought to the screen stay unless reverted with it: `_get_openable_manager_users` and `_get_openable_departments`, the builders `_get_manager_dashboard` and `_get_department_dashboard`, and the screen's refusal of the teams of a company outside the context.
