# A filer reads and comments on their own support requests in any company

Status: accepted (2026-11-19), amended (2026-11-20: read alone is taken off and given back, by the operation flags of an access)

The Support item of `project_support_request` files a request as a task of the project flagged *Develops This Odoo*, created with superuser rights in the filer's name. The task belongs to the company of that project. Odoo restricts every operation on a task to the companies ticked in the company switcher (`project.task_comp_rule`), so a filer who has not ticked that company, or is not allowed in it, files a request that the item then neither lists nor opens.

The requests stay in the company of the support project: the task of a project shares its company, and the project that develops this Odoo is one project for all companies. What changes is who reads them.

**A filer reads their own requests in any company, and comments on them.** From `project_support_request` 20.0.1.1.0 the company restriction on reading a task has one exception: a task of the project flagged *Develops This Odoo* of which the user is an internal UAT user. A task takes messages from its readers (`_mail_post_access = 'read'` on `project.task`), so the filer also comments on the request in its chatter. Creating, writing and deleting a task keep the plain company restriction. The user's company selection is kept for reading and opening: the item opens a request with a plain form action, and its list and *View Closed* show the request with whatever companies the user has ticked, whether or not the user is allowed in the company of the project. A save is the one moment the selection changes: a filer allowed in that company but not selecting it who saves a change to the request has the write refused, and the form of Odoo 20 answers by ticking the company, without a reload, and saving again (`onSaveError`, `web/static/src/views/form/form_controller.js`); the company stays ticked after it.

**How, in Odoo 20.** Access is a set of `ir.access` records. One with a group is a permission, and permissions are OR-ed; one without is a restriction, and every restriction is AND-ed with them. Core's `project.task_comp_rule` is such a restriction, for every operation, so no other record can widen it. The module takes read off it, and off the copies of it below, which leaves core's as shipped to create, update and delete (operation `cud`), and adds its own read restriction, `project_support_request.task_comp_rule_read`: the domain of core's restriction as it stands, or a task of the flagged project whose UAT users include the user as an internal user. The permissions are left as they are: a filer reads a request because they follow it, as core subscribes the creator of a task, which the filer is.

- The read restriction does not copy core's domain: its domain is `user._support_request_task_read_domain()`, a method evaluated when access is checked, as `project_member_access` builds its own. Core's restriction as it stands is every active restriction in its place, their domains AND-ed: core's record, and the copies Customize makes of it (below), by core's name at the start of theirs, as Customize names a copy `<name> (copy)`, or by core's domain. Core's name and domain are taken both from its row in `project/security/ir.access.csv`, or from the stock name and domain, constants of the module, when the file or the row cannot be read, and from the live record, the record's `id` too while it exists, by one function the read restriction, the module's data and the uninstall hook share: a copy stays recognised once the archived original is deleted, and a copy renamed after core's domain was edited in place is recognised by that domain. The read restriction follows only an access that covers update (`for_write`), so that reads follow what restricts writes: a create-only restriction named like core's, or with its domain, leaves reads alone while the module is installed. The same function holds that test, so the module's data narrows what the read restriction follows, and core's archived record with it. The uninstall leaves the test out: it gives read to every access taken for core's that lacks it, whatever else it covers and whether or not it ever had read, so an update-less copy gets read too (below). The data and the uninstall select by the flags of an access too (`for_read`), never by a whole operation, so a copy an administrator gave fewer operations is still found. Each domain is evaluated as core evaluates a restriction, with `ir.access`'s own evaluation context, in the company selection being checked: the user of that context keeps it. An administrator who edits core's domain, in place or in a copy, edits it for reads too, and Odoo's access cache, cleared on every change to an `ir.access`, picks the edit up. The module's read restriction and its copies, whose domain calls the method, are never taken for core's.
- An access in core's place that carries a group, core's or a copy an administrator gave one, is a permission: Odoo ORs it with the other permissions, so it restricts nobody, its members included, whom it grants instead. It fills core's place and adds nothing to the read restriction, and the module leaves its operation as it is, so its members keep what it grants them, read included if it covers read, as without the module.
- With nothing in core's place, core's archived or deleted without an active copy, reads keep the domain of core's row in `project/security/ir.access.csv`, or the stock domain when the file or the row cannot be read. The module lifts the company restriction of reads for its exception only: an administrator who lifts it for tasks altogether archives the module's read restriction as well.
- The module's data narrows on every install and update, with a function: it takes read, and read alone, off core's record, archived too, and off its active copies, all without a group and covering update; create, update and delete stay as they are, so a copy that a Customize made before the install with Delete unticked goes from `cru` to `cu`, while one with Update unticked keeps `crd` and, AND-ed by Odoo with the read restriction, goes on restricting reads, the exception's requests included. Core's record is taken archived too, here and by the uninstall, so that an administrator who unarchives it finds it with the operation the module last gave it. It logs what it narrowed. Installing restricts reads on a database that had lifted them before the install by unticking Read on core's restriction: the read restriction follows core's domain all the same, and an administrator who wants reads lifted archives it after installing. A function and not a record of core's: once an administrator has deleted core's record, such a record fails the update, as Odoo refuses to update a missing record of another module. An update of `project` writes core's `crud` back, and the update of the modules that depend on it, this one included, narrows it again in the same run.
- The uninstall hook gives read back, as Odoo ships core's restriction, to core's record, archived too, and to every active copy of it that lacks it, all without a group and whether or not they cover update; create, update and delete stay as an administrator left them, so a copy a Customize after the install left at `cu` gets `cru`, and one left at `cd` gets `crd`: with core's record archived, that copy left without read would leave the reads of tasks without a company restriction once the read restriction goes. It gives read whatever took it off, the module or an administrator, and to a matching access created without it too: the module does not record the operations of the restrictions before the install, so a database that had unticked Read on core's restriction before installing gets it back, and an administrator unticks it again afterwards. Core's record with a group is a permission and keeps its operation, as its copies with one do. It archives every access whose domain calls the module's method, on any model and with or without a group, and the copies Customize made of the module's read restriction, restrictions of tasks without a group whose name starts with the module's: such a domain stops evaluating once the method, or the fields of `project_ai_driven` it reads, are gone. It logs what it changed. The read restriction goes with the module.

**What the request's form reads.** The many2one names on the form (project, stage, company) are read by the web client as superuser. The chatter, its messages, followers and attachments follow the read access of the task, so they open. The project itself is not opened up: from the task, a user outside its company is refused the project, as before.

**No request is filed that its filer cannot open.** The route files a request for an internal user only, as their own UAT request in the flagged project, and they follow it: with the exception its filer reads it. The item opens a new request as it opens a listed one, and its routes answer no company and no *can open* with it. Two cases close a request to its filer all the same:

- The filer unfollows it. Following is what lets a base user read a task of a project they cannot read (`project.task_visibility_rule`), and the exception is a restriction: it lifts the company check and grants nothing.
- `project_member_access` is installed and the support project is Public, the one of its restricted modes the support project may take (it refuses Private there). That module's read restriction on the tasks of a Public or Private project, `('project_id', 'access', 'read')`, reads the project under the project's own company restriction, so a filer who does not select the company of the project is refused the request.

**Alternatives.**

- *Switch companies before opening*, as core suggests when a read is refused. It changes what the user sees on every screen after one click in the item, and it cannot help a user who is not allowed in the company of the project, short of allowing every filer in that company.
- *A notice only*: file the request and say it cannot be opened. The filer then cannot follow their own request: the item and *View Closed* do not list it.
- *Create the request in the filer's company.* A task takes the company of its project, and the decision keeps the requests in the support project's company.
- *A permission for the filer.* Permissions are OR-ed and the company restriction is AND-ed after them, so none can lift it.
- *An override of `project.task._access_domain`.* It works, but keeps the access out of the records Settings shows under *Accesses*, where an administrator reads and changes access.
- *Core's domain written into the module's read restriction.* An edit of core's restriction, in place or through Customize, then leaves reads on the stock domain while it changes create, update and delete.
- *No company restriction of reads when none stands in core's place*, as without the module. A copy of core's restriction both renamed and given another domain is not recognised, so that choice would open the tasks of every company to reads after an edit an administrator makes to restrict them.
- *An uninstall that gives each restriction back the operation it had before the install.* The install would have to record those operations, and a restriction an administrator edits or copies while the module is installed would be left with no record to go back to. The uninstall gives read back as Odoo ships it instead, and says so: an administrator who had unticked it before the install unticks it again.
- *A group on core's restriction restricting the reads of its members*, as a group rule did up to Odoo 16. Odoo 20 has no restriction with a group: the group makes the access a permission, which grants its members and restricts nobody.

**Consequences.**

- *Accesses* shows two company restrictions on tasks: core's, for create, update and delete, and the module's, for read.
- A filer outside the company of the project sees the form of their request with its fields editable, as any reader of a task does; saving a change is refused to a filer not allowed in that company, and ticks it for one who is (above).
- Portal and other share users are not covered: a portal UAT user of a request in another company is refused it, as before.
- *Accesses* edits core's restriction through Customize, which archives it and edits an active copy. Customised before the install, the copy carries read with whatever else the administrator left ticked, and the install takes read off it as off the original if it covers update; one that does not keeps read and goes on restricting reads, AND-ed by Odoo with the read restriction, so the exception does not apply until an administrator unticks Read on it. Customised after the install, the copy carries `cud`. Either way, while the copy covers update, the exception goes on applying and reads follow the copy's domain; a copy both renamed and given another domain is not recognised: customised before the install it goes on restricting reads, and the exception does not apply until an administrator narrows it to create, update and delete; customised after it, reads keep the domain of project's file (above). Either way the uninstall gives the copy read back, whether or not it covers update, unless it was both renamed and given another domain: such a copy keeps its operation, and one customised after the install leaves the reads of tasks without a company restriction until an administrator ticks *Read* on it again.

Reversal cost: low. Uninstalling the module gives read back to core's restriction; requests filed in the meantime stay where they are, in the company of the project, readable again to the users of that company only.
